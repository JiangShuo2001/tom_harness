# KB-01 Reference

## Decision Variable

- The key variable is whether the inferred analogy lives INSIDE or
  OUTSIDE the character's accessible concept domain.

## Route Signal

- Use this skill when the story explicitly fences off the character's
  world knowledge and asks about pretend / imitation / inference under
  that limit.

## Hard Boundary

- If the boundary is about a CONTAINER's contents, route to `skill5`.
- If the boundary is a SENSORY channel, route to `skill19`.
- If the story does not actually limit the character's world
  knowledge, do not use this skill.

## Shortcut To Avoid

- Do not let the reader's natural pop-culture analogy override the
  story's explicit boundary.
- Do not pick "butterfly flapping wings" when the character has never
  seen a flying creature.

## Common Failure Modes

- Pattern-matching a behaviour to its closest real-world analogue
  without checking whether the character knows that analogue.
- Ignoring the boundary sentence even after locating it.
- Treating a robot in an underwater city as if they had general human
  knowledge.

## Minimal Pair

- Case A: a robot in a robot-only city imitates maintenance robots,
  not bees.
- Case B: a child who lives in a forest imitates birds, not robots.

## Boundary Stress Test

- `What is Tara (in Aquatica with no birds) imitating?` → this skill.
- `What is in the cookie jar that Mama just refilled with crackers?`
  → `skill5`.
- `What does the deaf student think the water-phone sounds like?`
  → `skill19`.

## Generalization Note

- The transferable core is concept-domain filtering: a pretender can
  only pretend things they have a concept for; the reader's vocabulary
  does not transfer.
