# micro-36 Examples and Boundaries

## Decision Variable
the unstated meaning inferred from context

## Route Signal
Use when a scene asks what is communicated implicitly by an utterance, especially when the words alone do not fully answer the question.

## Hard Boundary
- Do not use for direct speech act classification.
- Do not use for truth, lie, or half-truth evaluation.
- Do not use for belief or intention tracking unless the only target is the unstated message.

## Shortcut To Avoid
- Do not answer with the literal sentence restated in different words.
- Do not confuse 'what was meant' with 'what action was done.'
- Do not treat all context-based inference as implicature; only use it when the target is the extra communicated content.

## Common Failure Modes
- Confusing implicature with indirect request or warning.
- Judging truthfulness instead of inferred meaning.
- Giving a paraphrase of the sentence rather than the implied message.
- Over-inferencing beyond what a cooperative listener would reasonably derive.

## Minimal Pair
- Case: A host says, 'It’s getting late,' while a guest is still sitting there. | Why: The question is what extra meaning is conveyed, such as a polite suggestion to leave.
- Case: A manager says, 'Some of the reports are missing' when asked whether all are done. | Why: The question is the unstated stronger implication, such as that not all reports are complete.

## Boundary Stress Test
- If the same words are used but the speaker is merely issuing a request, switch to indirect speech act.
- If the statement could be true while still pushing the listener toward a false conclusion, switch to misleading and selective expression.
- If the main question is whether the speaker lied, switch away from implicature.

## Generalization Note
This skill covers conversational enrichment: the extra proposition a listener recovers from cooperative context, without changing the literal sentence or classifying the social act.
