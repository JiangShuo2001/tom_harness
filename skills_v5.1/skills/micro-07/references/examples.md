# micro-07 Examples and Boundaries

## Decision Variable
whether the cue entered the character's attentional focus

## Route Signal
Use when the diagnostic question is whether a character actually noticed a cue, not whether the cue was present or meaningful.

## Hard Boundary
- Perceptual availability alone is not attention.
- Later recall does not prove initial noticing.
- Interpretation of the cue is outside this unit.

## Shortcut To Avoid
- Do not assume noticing just because the cue was obvious to the reader.
- Do not infer attention from a later reaction unless the reaction clearly shows initial registration.

## Common Failure Modes
- Confusing seeing with noticing.
- Confusing attention with memory.
- Confusing attention with inference about intention or belief.
- Using viewpoint rules when the real issue is distraction.

## Minimal Pair
- Case: A person glances at a flashing screen and then comments on it. | Why: The cue entered attention and was actively registered.
- Case: A person sits near a ringing phone but is absorbed in a loud argument and never reacts. | Why: The cue was perceptually available, but attention did not capture it.

## Boundary Stress Test
- Several characters can see the same event, but only one mentions it immediately.
- A cue is fully visible in the room, yet the target character is focused on a different task.
- A later statement reveals the character noticed something, but the scene never showed attention at the moment.

## Generalization Note
This skill isolates attentional selection: whether a cue became mentally foregrounded, not whether it was present, interpreted, remembered, or acted on.
