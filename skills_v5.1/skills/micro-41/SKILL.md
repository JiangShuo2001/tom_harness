# Persuasion Strategy

## Use When
- Use when the question asks how to change another person's belief, attitude, or behavior.
- Use when the key issue is the influence strategy directed at the listener.
- Use when the scene is about choosing what argument, framing, evidence, request, or tactic is most likely to persuade.

## Do Not Use When
- Do not use when the main issue is the speaker's motive for saying it; use speaker motivation instead.
- Do not use when the main issue is adapting language to the listener's age, expertise, identity, or stance without trying to change them; use audience calibration instead.
- Do not use when the situation is negotiation, compromise, or joint planning with mutual concessions; this unit is one-way influence, not bargaining.
- Do not use when the question is whether the statement is rude, offensive, or a faux pas; use faux pas and offense instead.
- Do not use when the question is who caused harm or deserves blame; use harm and responsibility instead.

## Decision Variable
the influence strategy directed at the listener

## Trigger Checklist
- Is the goal to change someone's mind, feeling, or action?
- Is the choice about which persuasive tactic will work best?
- Is the influence mostly one-way rather than a mutual exchange?
- Would the best answer name a strategy such as reason, emotion, authority, framing, reassurance, or social proof?

## Workflow
- Identify the target listener and the intended change.
- Ignore the speaker's private motive unless it changes the tactic choice.
- Check whether the interaction is one-way influence rather than negotiation.
- Select the strategy most likely to move the listener's belief, attitude, or behavior.

## Special Case
**Persuasion vs negotiation**: If both sides are making offers, trading concessions, or settling on a shared plan, route away from this unit. Persuasion strategy is for one person trying to change another person's response, not for mutual bargaining.

Scene: A manager wants an employee to accept a new schedule and is choosing whether to present safety data or offer extra time off.
Question: What strategy should the manager use?
Answer logic: This is persuasion strategy because the manager is selecting a one-way influence tactic to change the employee's response, not negotiating a mutual compromise.

## Boundary Exit Rule
- Exit to micro-39 if the task is to infer why the speaker spoke that way rather than how to change the listener.
- Exit to micro-40 if the scene is primarily about tailoring wording to the audience's knowledge, identity, or comfort level rather than persuading them.
- Exit to micro-51 if the interaction contains reciprocal concessions, a deal, or shared decision-making.
- Exit to micro-42 or micro-43 if the key judgment is tact, offense, harm, blame, or repair.
