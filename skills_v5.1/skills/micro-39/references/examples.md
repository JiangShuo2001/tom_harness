# micro-39 Examples and Boundaries

## Decision Variable
the speaker's social motive for the utterance

## Route Signal
Use this micro unit when the prompt asks for the speaker's motive behind a particular utterance, especially for politeness, face management, avoidance, testing, or ingratiation.

## Hard Boundary
- Truth or lie judgment
- Persuasion strategy
- Audience calibration
- Indirect meaning or implied request

## Shortcut To Avoid
- Do not confuse 'why said this way' with 'is it true'.
- Do not turn any social phrasing into persuasion unless the goal is clearly to change the listener.
- Do not infer audience adaptation just because the wording is careful or simple.

## Common Failure Modes
- Answering with truth status instead of motive.
- Treating polite wording as persuasion.
- Treating hedging as audience calibration without evidence.
- Treating indirect speech as speaker motivation when the question asks for the literal intended meaning.

## Minimal Pair
- Case: A host says, 'Please take the last slice,' while actually hoping the guest will feel welcomed and comfortable. | Why: The question is about the social motive behind the wording: politeness and hospitality.
- Case: A salesperson says, 'Most experts already use this model,' to convince a customer to buy it. | Why: Near miss: the wording is chosen to change the listener's decision, so the central variable is the influence tactic. Route to persuasion strategy.

## Boundary Stress Test
- If the same sentence is true, false, or misleading, but the question asks why it was said, stay here.
- If the same sentence is chosen to make a child understand it, route to audience calibration.
- If the same sentence is chosen to convince someone to act, route to persuasion strategy.
- If the same sentence is chosen to hide facts or create a false impression, route to lie or misleading expression.
- If the same sentence is a disguised request or refusal, route to indirect language.

## Generalization Note
This unit generalizes to any utterance where the key variable is the speaker's social reason for choosing that form, not the factual content or the listener's comprehension.
