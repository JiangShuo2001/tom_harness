# macro-05 Examples and Boundaries

## Decision Variable
why a character's true feeling differs from outward display or ordinary expectation

## Route Signal
A mismatch between what a character shows and what they likely feel, especially when the feeling is concealed, reversed, or atypical for the situation.

## Hard Boundary
- Moral emotion and responsibility judgment
- Appearance-reality and knowledge boundary
- Social cue and nonverbal intention
- Indirect language and conversational meaning

## Shortcut To Avoid
- Do not answer from the visible expression alone.
- Do not map the situation to the usual emotion if the context strongly supports a hidden or reversed reaction.
- Do not convert the problem into blame or fault just because the scene contains harm or awkwardness.

## Common Failure Modes
- Confusing polite masking with genuine feeling.
- Assuming the expected emotion instead of the contextually plausible one.
- Drifting into moral judgment when the question only asks for emotion.
- Treating a social cue as if it were a literal statement about feeling.

## Minimal Pair
- Case: A student smiles and says 'I'm fine' after failing a test, but later looks crushed when alone. | Why: The visible calm masks the real emotion, so the task is to infer hidden disappointment.
- Case: A parent looks angry after a child spills juice, but the question asks whether the parent blames the child. | Why: The scene is about visible emotion, but the actual target is blame and responsibility, which belongs outside this macro.

## Boundary Stress Test
- If the person says one thing and feels another, decide whether the issue is hidden emotion or indirect language.
- If the character feels something unusual for the situation, check whether the emotion is still the main target rather than the action or belief.
- If the scene includes harm or embarrassment, test whether the answer requires guilt or shame instead of hidden feeling.

## Generalization Note
This macro generalizes across cases where the solver must recover private emotion from social masking, emotional reversal, or atypical appraisal without relying on a single benchmark pattern.
