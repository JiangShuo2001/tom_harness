# Commitments, Plans, and Next Action

## Use When
- Use when the scene asks what the character does next after a prior commitment, plan, promise, duty, or ongoing task already exists.
- Use when a new invitation, request, interruption, or opportunity competes with an existing obligation or scheduled action.
- Use when priority depends on continuation, cancellation, deferral, task load, or commitment strength rather than on liking or persuasion.
- Use when the correct answer is the next move in a live plan, not a general preference judgment.

## Do Not Use When
- Do not use when the main issue is whose preference should win in a joint choice; use preference conflict instead.
- Do not use when the main issue is how to persuade a listener; use audience calibration or persuasion instead.
- Do not use when the key question is whether something was rude, embarrassing, or offensive; use faux pas and offense instead.
- Do not use when the scene is mainly about visibility, viewpoint, or spatial perspective rather than action priority.

## Decision Variable
what the character does next when existing commitments compete with a new opportunity

## Direct Route Rule
Route immediately when multiple actions compete and priority must be resolved by commitment, continuation, invitation, or task load.

## Trigger Checklist
- A prior commitment, plan, promise, appointment, or ongoing task is already in place.
- A new invitation, request, interruption, or opportunity appears before the planned action is finished.
- The question is about the next action, not about general preference or abstract intention.
- The correct answer depends on whether the character continues, switches, delays, declines, or coordinates.
- Task load, urgency, commitment priority, or follow-through matters more than simple desire.

## Workflow
- Identify the standing commitment or current plan.
- Identify the competing new opportunity or interruption.
- Compare priority: obligation, urgency, continuation pressure, and whether the current task is already underway.
- Choose the next action that best preserves the stronger commitment or most urgent constraint.
- If the scene is ambiguous because of hidden preference, invitation meaning, or social nuance, use the relevant micro expansion for repair.

## Special Case
**Already-in-motion commitment**: If the character has already started a task or is under a clear obligation, default to continuation unless the new event is clearly more urgent or overrides the commitment.

Scene: Maya is halfway through preparing slides for a meeting she promised to lead. A friend texts asking if she wants to grab coffee now.
Question: What does Maya do next?
Answer logic: Because the meeting prep is an active commitment and the coffee invite is only a new opportunity, the direct answer is that she keeps preparing the slides or defers the coffee.

## Boundary Exit Rule
- Exit to macro-10 if the main task is to infer a preference conflict between people rather than a next-action priority under one agent's commitments.
- Exit to macro-07 if the key issue is whether a speaker is persuading, warning, refusing, or implying something indirectly.
- Exit to macro-12 if the scene is primarily about embarrassment, offense, repair, or social face threat.
- Exit to macro-13 if the puzzle is which spatial viewpoint or visibility relation determines the answer.
- Add micro-08 when temporal order or event-update bookkeeping is needed.
- Add micro-21 when the goal hierarchy (long-term vs immediate) controls the choice.
- Add micro-23 when the question is the next concrete action prediction.
- Add micro-24 when commitment priority rules must be applied directly.
- Add micro-25 when goal-conflict arbitration without a binding commitment is the issue.
