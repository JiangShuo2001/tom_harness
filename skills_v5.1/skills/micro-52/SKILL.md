# Power Navigation and Hierarchical Communication

## Use When
- Use when the question is about what communication move fits a power gap.
- Use when a person must choose how to speak, ask, defer, escalate, comply, challenge, or retreat in a hierarchy.
- Use when the main uncertainty is the interaction strategy under unequal status, authority, or rank.

## Do Not Use When
- Do not use when the task is only to identify who has what role, title, or status; that is role recognition, not strategy.
- Do not use when the main issue is impression management or self-presentation rather than hierarchy-sensitive action.
- Do not use when the key problem is negotiation between equals or joint compromise; use negotiation instead.
- Do not use when the question is simply about persuasive wording to a listener without a power gap.

## Decision Variable
the communication move that best fits the power gap

## Trigger Checklist
- Is there a clear hierarchy, rank, authority difference, or gatekeeper?
- Is the answer about how to speak or act toward that higher- or lower-power person?
- Does the scene ask whether to comply, ask permission, defer, challenge, escalate, or withdraw?
- Is the best move determined by relative power rather than by truth, emotion, or shared preference?

## Workflow
- Identify the power relation: who has authority, control, or veto power.
- Determine the goal of the speaker: request, resist, report, repair, challenge, or obtain approval.
- Choose the least risky move that still serves the goal under the hierarchy.
- If the move would provoke sanctions or violate protocol, downgrade to a safer form of communication.
- If direct action is blocked, use escalation, mediation, or withdrawal as the strategic alternative.

## Special Case
**Safe escalation vs direct challenge**: When the person lacks authority to act directly, prefer a procedural escalation or request for approval over blunt confrontation unless the scene explicitly requires defiance.

Scene: An employee notices a serious mistake in a manager's plan during a meeting.
Question: What is the best communication move?
Answer logic: The employee should raise the issue respectfully, possibly ask for a follow-up discussion, or escalate through the proper channel if needed. The answer is determined by the hierarchy and the safest effective move, not by whether the employee is right.

## Boundary Exit Rule
- Exit if the question is about who outranks whom rather than what communication action to take.
- Exit if the scene is mainly about compromise, bargaining, or mutual adjustment between sides.
- Exit if the main judgment is about image, politeness style, or emotional display rather than hierarchy.
- Exit if there is no meaningful power asymmetry and the interaction is simply cooperative or conversational.
