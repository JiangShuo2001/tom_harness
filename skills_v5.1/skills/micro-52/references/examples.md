# micro-52 Examples and Boundaries

## Decision Variable
the communication move that best fits the power gap

## Route Signal
Use this micro unit when a scene asks how to communicate across a status gap, especially when the choice is among defer, comply, ask, challenge, escalate, or retreat.

## Hard Boundary
- Not role labeling; it is about strategy under hierarchy.
- Not general persuasion; it requires a power asymmetry.
- Not negotiation; it does not require bilateral trade-offs.

## Shortcut To Avoid
- Do not answer from fairness alone without checking who has authority.
- Do not confuse respectful wording with strategic deference.
- Do not treat a request to a superior as the same as persuading an equal.

## Common Failure Modes
- Overusing direct challenge when a deferential or procedural move is safer.
- Confusing rank recognition with communication strategy.
- Missing that escalation is sometimes the best move when direct action is blocked.
- Solving the scene as compromise when the hierarchy constrains the options.

## Minimal Pair
- Case: A student asks a professor for an extension because of illness. | Why: The student must choose a hierarchy-appropriate request and tone.
- Case: A team member and peer disagree on a shared plan and need a compromise. | Why: This is bilateral negotiation, not power-gap communication.

## Boundary Stress Test
- If the same message is sent to a boss, a peer, or a subordinate, does the best move change because of power?
- If the scene only asks who is in charge, this unit should not be used.
- If a polite reply would be fine with an equal but not with a superior, the power gap is decisive.

## Generalization Note
This skill generalizes to workplace, family, institutional, and status-based interactions where the key question is how to communicate strategically under hierarchy.
