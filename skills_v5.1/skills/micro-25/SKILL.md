# Goal-Conflict Arbitration

## Use When
- Use when the question asks which of two or more active goals matters more to the character.
- Use when the task is to identify the weighting rule behind a choice among competing wants, motives, or aims.
- Use when the scene does not depend on a prior commitment, fixed schedule, or explicit obligation.

## Do Not Use When
- Do not use when the question is really about the next action the character will take; use action prediction instead.
- Do not use when an existing commitment, ongoing plan, or invitation sets the priority; use commitment priority instead.
- Do not use when the issue is emotion from success, loss, threat, or blame; use an emotion-appraisal skill instead.
- Do not use when the question is about intention recognition, persuasion, or social meaning rather than goal weighting.

## Decision Variable
which goal gets greater weight in the character's prioritization rule

## Trigger Checklist
- Two or more goals are active at the same time.
- The question asks which goal wins, outweighs, or dominates.
- No fixed commitment already settles the choice.
- The answer depends on priority, salience, urgency, reward, cost, or value tradeoff.

## Workflow
- Identify all competing goals that are active in the scene.
- Check what the question asks to compare: priority, strength, urgency, payoff, or moral cost.
- Infer the weighting rule the character is using.
- Choose the goal that the rule makes dominant.

## Special Case
**Tie or near-tie between goals**: If the scene gives no commitment and no explicit priority cue, use the strongest stated incentive, threat, or immediate payoff to break the tie.

Scene: Dana wants to leave early to meet a friend, but also wants to finish a task before the meeting.
Question: Which goal wins?
Answer logic: Compare the active goals and look for the stronger stated weight. If the task has a deadline or major cost, it outweighs the meeting; if not, the social plan may win. The key variable is the weighting rule, not the exact action sequence.

## Boundary Exit Rule
- Exit immediately to micro-23 Action Prediction if the question asks what the character will do next rather than how goals are weighted.
- Exit immediately to micro-24 Commitment Priority if an existing obligation, plan, or invitation determines the answer.
- Exit immediately to micro-21 Goal Hierarchy if the question asks how local goals nest under broader goals rather than which one outweighs the other.
- Exit immediately to an emotion-appraisal unit (micro-26 to micro-31) if the scene is about how the character feels about gain, loss, threat, blame, or success.
- Exit immediately to micro-22 or a belief unit if the scene is about intention reading or belief inference without active goal competition.
