# Vague Quantity Prior

## Use When
- Use when the task asks what a vague quantifier such as "some," "many," "most," "few," "almost none," "about half," or "nearly all" implies.
- Use when the answer depends on the listener's ordinary prior interpretation of a quantity phrase, not on later observations.
- Use when a character's expectation or social interpretation hinges on whether a phrase suggests a small minority, a large minority, a majority, or near-total amount.
- Use when estimating an approximate range is enough and an exact count is not available or not intended.

## Do Not Use When
- Do not use when the estimate must be revised after seeing a sample, receiving new counts, or observing outcomes; route to micro-54.
- Do not use when the main question is category likelihood, typicality, or base-rate probability rather than the meaning of a quantity phrase; route to micro-55.
- Do not use when vague quantity language is part of bargaining, concession, or a joint plan; route to micro-51 for the negotiation move.
- Do not use when the issue is how to phrase a message across a hierarchy or power gap; route to micro-52.
- Do not use when an exact arithmetic answer is required and the vague expression is irrelevant.

## Decision Variable
the approximate range implied by the vague quantifier

## Trigger Checklist
- A vague quantifier or approximate quantity phrase is present.
- The question asks what amount, fraction, or expectation the phrase suggests.
- No new observation needs to update the estimate.
- The answer can be expressed as an approximate range or ordinal quantity category.
- Speaker intention, power, or negotiation is not the primary variable.

## Workflow
- Identify the vague quantity phrase and the reference set it applies to.
- Decide whether the phrase implies count, proportion, frequency, or intensity.
- Map the phrase to an approximate qualitative range, such as near zero, small minority, moderate minority, about half, majority, large majority, or near all.
- Adjust lightly for context only when the reference set changes the practical meaning, such as "a few people" versus "a few cities."
- Keep the answer as a prior interpretation unless the prompt gives later evidence that must update it.
- State the implied range and avoid inventing exact numbers unless the question explicitly requests a rough numeric example.

## Special Case
**Context-sensitive but not evidence-updated**: A vague quantifier may shift with the size and stakes of the reference class, but it should not be revised using later sample evidence within this unit.

Scene: A teacher says that most students submitted the form before the deadline. No roster count is given.
Question: What should the listener assume about the number of students who submitted it?
Answer logic: The phrase "most students" implies more than half and usually a clear majority, not necessarily all. Because no later observation or sample is provided, the answer stays at the prior interpretation of "most."

## Boundary Exit Rule
- Exit to micro-54 if the prompt says someone observed a subset, sample, count, or outcome and asks how the estimate should change.
- Exit to micro-55 if the prompt asks which category is more likely because it is common, typical, or rare, rather than what a quantifier means.
- Exit to micro-51 if the vague quantity is an offer, concession, or compromise term whose social function matters more than its range.
- Exit to micro-52 if the vague quantity is chosen mainly to manage deference, authority, escalation, or compliance.
- Exit if the task requires exact calculation from explicit numbers instead of interpreting a vague phrase.
