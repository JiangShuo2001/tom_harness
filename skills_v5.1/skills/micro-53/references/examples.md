# micro-53 Examples and Boundaries

## Decision Variable
the approximate range implied by the vague quantifier

## Route Signal
Use this micro unit when the central task is to translate a vague quantity expression into its ordinary approximate range before any evidence update.

## Hard Boundary
- This unit is about semantic-pragmatic quantity priors, not Bayesian or sample-based updating.
- This unit does not decide whether a person is reliable, strategic, polite, or deceptive unless that depends only on the implied quantity range.
- This unit does not solve negotiation, authority, or relationship strategy scenes even if they contain words like "some" or "most."

## Shortcut To Avoid
- Do not convert every vague quantifier into a fixed exact percentage; preserve approximate ranges.
- Do not let later evidence leak backward into the meaning of the original phrase.
- Do not treat "some" as necessarily "only a few"; it can mean more than one but less than all, with context shaping the expected range.

## Common Failure Modes
- Answering with an exact number when only an approximate interpretation is licensed.
- Updating the estimate after a sample and incorrectly staying in this unit.
- Confusing typicality judgments, such as whether a bird is likely to fly, with vague quantity interpretation.
- Ignoring the reference class, such as interpreting "a few errors" the same way in a one-page note and a million-line database.

## Minimal Pair
- Case: A sign says "Most seats are taken." The question asks how full the room probably is. | Why: The task is to interpret the vague quantifier "most" as a prior range, likely a clear majority of seats.
- Case: A sign says "Most seats are taken," but then Sam looks inside and sees only 3 of 20 seats occupied. The question asks what Sam should now believe. | Why: This is a near miss for this unit because the later observation changes the estimate; route to micro-54.

## Boundary Stress Test
- If the phrase is "almost none," answer near zero but not necessarily exactly zero.
- If the phrase is "about half," allow a range around half rather than exactly 50%.
- If the phrase is "some," avoid assuming majority unless context strongly supports it.
- If the phrase is "nearly all," distinguish it from "all" because exceptions remain possible.
- If explicit sample evidence appears after the phrase, stop using this unit and update through micro-54.

## Generalization Note
Vague quantifiers encode socially shared approximate expectations; the key is to recover the ordinary range implied by the wording while keeping it separate from later evidence, exact arithmetic, and strategic communication goals.
