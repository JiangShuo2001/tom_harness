# micro-54 Examples and Boundaries

## Decision Variable
sample-driven quantity revision

## Route Signal
Use this micro unit when partial sampled evidence should change an estimate about a larger unobserved quantity.

## Hard Boundary
- This unit is about updating from observed samples, not merely knowing that a category is common or rare.
- This unit is about quantity estimation, not causal responsibility, blame, or motive.
- This unit does not decide whether a sample was morally fair or socially appropriate; it only assesses evidential force for a quantity estimate.

## Shortcut To Avoid
- Do not assume the observed sample exactly equals the whole population.
- Do not ignore sample size: two observed cases should not outweigh a large known pattern unless the context makes them highly diagnostic.
- Do not treat a vivid or recent observation as representative without checking how it was selected.

## Common Failure Modes
- Overgeneralizing from a tiny sample.
- Failing to update at all when a meaningful sample contradicts the prior expectation.
- Confusing base-rate reasoning with sample-based revision.
- Treating biased samples as random samples.
- Answering with an exact count when the unobserved remainder is still unknown.

## Minimal Pair
- Case: A person checks 10 boxes from a shipment of 100 and finds 8 damaged, then estimates how many boxes may be damaged overall. | Why: The estimate about the whole shipment is revised using a partial observed sample.
- Case: A person says 'most boxes are usually fine' because shipments from this company are usually reliable. | Why: This is a near miss for micro-54 but actually belongs to base rate and typicality reasoning because no new sample was observed.

## Boundary Stress Test
- If the sample is large and randomly chosen, the estimate should move strongly toward the sample proportion.
- If the sample is small, selected from an unusual subgroup, or chosen after noticing a problem, the estimate should move only weakly.
- If a vague phrase and an observed sample both appear, first interpret the phrase only as a prior, then let the sample evidence revise it.
- If the question asks 'who caused the increase' rather than 'how many are likely,' leave this unit.

## Generalization Note
The same reasoning applies to social and non-social estimates: attendance, agreement, defect rates, likely votes, remembered events, or how many people in a group know something, as long as the key operation is updating a quantity from partial observation.
