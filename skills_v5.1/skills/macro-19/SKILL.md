# First Impression, Self-Presentation, and Formal Interaction

## Use When
- Use when the main task is to manage how one is seen by others.
- Use when the scene centers on first impressions, self-presentation, etiquette, or polished social presentation.
- Use when the answer depends on choosing wording, tone, style, posture, or behavior to create a desired impression.
- Use when the scene is a direct prototype of formal interaction that is not primarily about hierarchy, repair, or negotiation.

## Do Not Use When
- Do not use when the main issue is apology, compensation, reconciliation, or relationship repair after harm.
- Do not use when the main issue is bargaining, compromise, or a jointly optimized plan between conflicting sides.
- Do not use when the main issue is communication across a power gap, escalation, deference, or challenge to authority.
- Do not use when the question is mainly about hidden emotion, truthfulness, or misunderstanding rather than self-presentation.

## Decision Variable
how to manage the image one presents to others

## Direct Route Rule
Route immediately when first impressions, self-presentation, or formal interaction style are central.

## Trigger Checklist
- A new social setting where impressions matter.
- The speaker must choose how formal, polished, reserved, or personable to seem.
- The question asks what to say, wear, do, or avoid to look appropriate.
- Audience expectations shape the correct response.
- The goal is impression management rather than repair, negotiation, or hierarchy management.
- The scene is about presenting a stable social self-image.

## Workflow
- Identify the audience and the image the person wants to project.
- Determine which behavior best supports that image in this setting.
- Prefer tone, wording, and conduct that match the intended first impression.
- Answer in terms of the most socially fitting presentation choice, not hidden motives or later repair.
- If the case shifts to apology, bargaining, or hierarchy, exit to the relevant macro.

## Special Case
**Formal does not mean power-gap**: Use this macro when formality is chosen to manage presentation, not because the scene is mainly about rank, authority, or escalation.

Scene: A person is meeting a future employer for the first time and wants to seem professional and trustworthy.
Question: How should they greet the employer?
Answer logic: Choose the greeting that creates a polished first impression: respectful, concise, and appropriate to the setting. The decision is driven by self-presentation, so this routes here directly.

## Boundary Exit Rule
- Exit to macro-17 if the main issue becomes apology, compensation, or reconciliation after offense.
- Exit to macro-18 if the main issue becomes offers, concessions, or a mutually acceptable compromise.
- Exit to macro-20 if the main issue becomes deference, escalation, authority, or cross-level communication.
- Exit to macro-05 / macro-01 if the question is really about what someone feels, knows, or intends rather than how they present themselves.
- Add micro-29 when display-rule masking is the active lever.
- Add micro-39 when the speaker's social motive behind the wording must be diagnosed.
- Add micro-40 when audience calibration to expertise, identity, or stance is needed.
- Add micro-50 for fine-grained self-presentation diagnostics (disclosure order, framing, identity signaling).
