# Negotiation, Concession, and Joint Plan

## Use When
- Use when the task asks who must adjust, concede, trade, or combine preferences to reach agreement.
- Use when both sides retain some needs and the answer depends on how each side changes to form a joint solution.
- Use when the key question is the shape of the compromise, not whether one speaker should persuade the other.

## Do Not Use When
- Do not use when only one person is changing their view, because that is persuasion, advice, or influence rather than negotiation.
- Do not use when the scene is mainly about apology, repair, forgiveness, or easing hurt after harm.
- Do not use when the main issue is hierarchy, authority, compliance, or deference instead of mutual adjustment.
- Do not use when the question is about self-presentation, public image, or impression management rather than agreement design.

## Decision Variable
how both sides adjust to form a joint solution

## Trigger Checklist
- Two parties have different preferences or demands.
- Each side can give up something, trade something, or accept a partial match.
- The answer depends on compromise rather than a single correct choice.
- The scene asks for an agreed plan, split, or compromise outcome.

## Workflow
- Identify each side's non-negotiable wants and flexible wants.
- Check whether both sides can change terms without one side simply submitting.
- Find the overlap or trade-off that satisfies enough of both sides.
- Choose the option that is jointly acceptable, not merely persuasive or polite.

## Special Case
**Concession without surrender**: A concession counts as negotiation only if the other side also changes terms, accepts a trade, or gains part of what they want.

Scene: Two coworkers want the same meeting room. One can move to a later time if the other shares equipment.
Question: What is the best social-cognitive interpretation?
Answer logic: This is negotiation because both people adjust terms: one changes time, the other gives equipment access. The answer rests on bilateral concession, not one-sided persuasion.

## Boundary Exit Rule
- Exit to persuasion if one person is simply trying to make the other agree without reciprocal adjustment.
- Exit to repair if the central issue is apology, blame, hurt, or restoring trust after a social violation.
- Exit to power navigation if one side has authority and the main move is command, permission, escalation, or compliance.
- Exit to self-presentation if the main issue is what image to project rather than what agreement to reach.
