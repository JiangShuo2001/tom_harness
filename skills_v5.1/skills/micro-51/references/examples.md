# micro-51 Examples and Boundaries

## Decision Variable
how both sides adjust to form a joint solution

## Route Signal
Use this micro unit when a scene asks how two sides shape a mutually acceptable plan through compromise, trades, or concessions.

## Hard Boundary
- Mutual adjustment is required; one-sided influence alone does not qualify.
- The target is the structure of agreement, not the content of a final purchase, promise, or preference.
- If apology or relationship repair is the main social work, route elsewhere.

## Shortcut To Avoid
- Do not assume every disagreement is negotiation; many are persuasion or refusal.
- Do not answer from fairness alone; the key is whether both sides change terms.
- Do not confuse polite phrasing with actual concession.

## Common Failure Modes
- Treating a compromise scene as simple persuasion.
- Choosing the option that favors one side completely.
- Missing a hidden trade-off that makes the plan jointly acceptable.
- Confusing agreement-making with apology or reassurance.

## Minimal Pair
- Case: One friend says, 'Please come to my movie,' and keeps pushing until the other agrees. | Why: This is not negotiation if only one side is trying to sway the other; it is persuasion.
- Case: Two roommates split chores by each taking the task they dislike least. | Why: This matches negotiation because both sides adjust preferences to reach a mutually acceptable division.

## Boundary Stress Test
- If one person gives in and the other does not adjust at all, do not call it negotiation.
- If the scene includes an apology but no trade or compromise, do not call it negotiation.
- If the answer depends on rank, permission, or command, route away from this unit.

## Generalization Note
This unit generalizes to any social exchange where the diagnostic variable is reciprocal concession toward a shared plan, across friendships, work, family, and resource conflicts.
