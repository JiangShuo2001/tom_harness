# Emotion Regulation

## Use When
- Use when the question asks how a character changes, reduces, contains, redirects, justifies, or soothes their own emotional state.
- Use when the key issue is an internal coping process after an emotion has been triggered.
- Use when the answer depends on strategies such as calming down, distracting oneself, reframing the situation, rationalizing, self-talk, avoidance for coping, or seeking comfort to feel better.
- Use when the character may still feel the emotion, but is actively trying to manage its intensity, direction, or meaning.

## Do Not Use When
- Do not use when the main question is only what emotion the character feels; route to an emotion-appraisal unit instead.
- Do not use when the character is merely hiding an emotion for politeness or social display while the internal feeling is unchanged.
- Do not use when the focus is the literal or indirect meaning of what someone said rather than how the speaker regulates their feelings.
- Do not use when the emotion arises mainly from comparing reality with an alternative outcome, unless the question then asks how the character copes with that emotion.
- Do not use when one person's emotion simply spreads to another person; route to empathy or emotional contagion unless the second person then manages their own state.

## Decision Variable
the character's internal emotion-management strategy

## Trigger Checklist
- A character has an identifiable emotional reaction or emotional pressure.
- The question asks what the character does mentally or behaviorally to feel calmer, safer, less upset, less guilty, more hopeful, or more in control.
- There is evidence of coping, reappraisal, suppression, distraction, self-comfort, rationalization, venting, or redirection.
- The answer depends on internal regulation, not just public expression.
- The regulated emotion belongs to the same character whose strategy is being analyzed.

## Workflow
- Identify the character's initial emotional trigger and likely raw emotion.
- Separate internal feeling from external display: ask whether the character is changing the feeling itself or only managing what others see.
- Look for the regulation method: suppression, reappraisal, distraction, avoidance, self-comfort, rationalization, problem-focused action, or seeking support.
- Infer the intended effect of the strategy: reduce intensity, redirect attention, preserve self-image, regain control, tolerate distress, or transform the meaning of the event.
- Answer with the regulation strategy and its emotional function, not with a broad personality judgment.

## Special Case
**Private composure after social or moral discomfort**: If a character feels guilt, shame, embarrassment, anger, or disappointment and then privately reframes, excuses, calms, distracts, or comforts themselves, classify the key variable as emotion regulation even if the trigger is moral or social.

Scene: Maya snaps at a coworker during a meeting. Afterward she feels guilty, tells herself she was under pressure, takes a walk, and decides to apologize later.
Question: How is Maya managing her emotion?
Answer logic: The focus is not only that Maya feels guilty. She is reducing and organizing the guilt through rationalization, calming behavior, and a repair plan, so the relevant strategy is internal emotion regulation.

## Boundary Exit Rule
- Exit to micro-31 when the decisive clue is regret, relief, disappointment, or surprise from comparing what happened with what could have happened, and no coping strategy is being asked.
- Exit to micro-32 when the key process is absorbing or mirroring another person's emotion, not managing one's own emotion after it arises.
- Exit to micro-34 when the task is to state the explicit content of an utterance.
- Exit to micro-35 when the task is to identify what social act an utterance performs, such as a request, warning, refusal, or hint.
- Exit to a hidden-emotion or display-rule unit when the only issue is whether the character shows or conceals an emotion outwardly, with no evidence of internal regulation.
