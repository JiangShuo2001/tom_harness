# micro-33 Examples and Boundaries

## Decision Variable
the character's internal emotion-management strategy

## Route Signal
Use this micro unit when the scene turns on how a character copes with, changes, contains, redirects, or reinterprets their own emotional state.

## Hard Boundary
- Internal regulation is required; outward politeness or masking alone is not enough.
- The unit does not decide the basic emotion unless that is needed to identify the regulation strategy.
- The unit does not interpret speech acts unless the utterance is used as self-talk or coping evidence.
- The unit does not cover pure empathy unless the empathic emotion is then actively regulated.

## Shortcut To Avoid
- Do not assume a calm face means the character feels calm internally.
- Do not label every explanation as rationalization; check whether it functions to reduce or reshape emotion.
- Do not confuse solving the external problem with regulating the internal emotional response, unless the action is chosen to manage distress.

## Common Failure Modes
- Answering with the emotion label only, such as 'sad' or 'angry,' instead of the regulation process.
- Treating social display rules as internal emotion regulation.
- Ignoring self-talk, distraction, or reappraisal because they are subtle.
- Overreading regulation when the character simply moves on with no evidence of coping.
- Confusing moral responsibility with emotion regulation when the question is actually about blame or guilt attribution.

## Minimal Pair
- Case: Lena is furious but takes slow breaths and reminds herself that the mistake was accidental so she will not stay angry. | Why: The question centers on Lena changing the intensity and interpretation of her own anger through calming and reappraisal.
- Case: Lena is furious but smiles at the customer because her manager is watching. | Why: This does not match if the question is only about public masking; the evidence concerns external display rather than internal regulation.

## Boundary Stress Test
- If a character says 'I'm fine' while privately still upset, do not route here unless there is evidence they are trying to become fine internally.
- If a character feels relieved because the worse outcome did not happen, route to counterfactual emotion unless the question asks how they calm down afterward.
- If a character comforts a crying friend, do not route here unless the question asks how the helper manages their own distress.
- If a character says 'Could you lower your voice?' decide whether the target is the indirect request itself or the speaker's attempt to reduce their own anxiety.

## Generalization Note
Emotion regulation generalizes across private thoughts, bodily calming, attention shifts, meaning-making, avoidance, self-justification, and support seeking, as long as the core variable is how the character manages their own internal emotion.
