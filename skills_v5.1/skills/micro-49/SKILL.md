# Relationship Repair and Apology

## Use When
- Use when the task is to judge whether an apology, explanation, compensation, reassurance, or other easing action is needed after a relationship has been strained.
- Use when the key question is the repair need after relationship damage, not whether the original act was rude, harmful, or embarrassing.
- Use when the scene already contains a hurt, offense, misunderstanding, betrayal, or awkward interaction and asks for the follow-up response.

## Do Not Use When
- Do not use when the main question is whether the original act was a faux pas, offense, or embarrassment in the first place.
- Do not use when the main issue is privacy, secret-keeping, self-presentation, or disclosure timing rather than repair after harm.
- Do not use when the main issue is negotiation, compromise, or choosing a joint plan without relational damage.
- Do not use when no relationship has been damaged and the scene is only about general politeness or emotion display.

## Decision Variable
the amount of repair needed after relationship damage

## Trigger Checklist
- Was someone hurt, offended, excluded, embarrassed, disappointed, or misled?
- Is the question asking what should happen next to mend the relationship?
- Does the scene involve apology, explanation, restitution, reassurance, or easing tension?
- Is the answer about repair level rather than blame detection or norm judgment?

## Workflow
- Confirm that some relational damage has already occurred.
- Identify the damage type: hurt feelings, offense, misunderstanding, betrayal, or awkwardness.
- Judge whether repair is needed and how strong it should be: apology only, explanation, compensation, reassurance, or continued follow-up.
- Prefer the least repair that would credibly restore trust or ease strain.

## Special Case
**Silent repair after minor harm**: A repair can be needed even when no explicit apology is requested; a softening move, acknowledgment, or small compensation may still be the correct answer.

Scene: A person forgets a friend's birthday and later realizes the friend is distant and upset.
Question: What should they do to repair things?
Answer logic: The damage already happened, so the task is not to judge the mistake itself but to identify the needed repair: acknowledge the oversight, apologize, and possibly make amends.

## Boundary Exit Rule
- If the question is whether the act itself was rude, inappropriate, or embarrassing, exit to faux pas or offense judgment.
- If the question is about what information should have been shared or hidden, exit to privacy or disclosure.
- If the question is about how to persuade, negotiate, or choose a joint plan, exit to those action-structure units.
- If no relationship damage is present, do not force repair reasoning.
