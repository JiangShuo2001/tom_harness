# micro-24 Examples and Boundaries

## Decision Variable
the priority order among commitments or plans

## Route Signal
Use this micro unit when the scene involves competing obligations, a schedule conflict, or a choice between an existing commitment and a new request.

## Hard Boundary
- Not for general preferences with no commitment structure.
- Not for future action prediction unless the ranking of commitments is the core reason.
- Not for intention reading, emotion appraisal, or norm violation by itself.

## Shortcut To Avoid
- Do not assume the strongest desire wins; commitment priority is not the same as preference strength.
- Do not infer the next action from mood or personality when the question is about obligation order.
- Do not treat any two goals as equal competitors if one is already promised or scheduled.

## Common Failure Modes
- Confusing commitment priority with simple goal conflict.
- Answering with the most enjoyable or most urgent-seeming option instead of the most binding one.
- Using intention inference when the scene only asks for ranking of plans.

## Minimal Pair
- Case: The character promised to babysit at 6 p.m. and then gets a casual invitation to dinner. | Why: Two plans compete, and the existing promise determines priority.
- Case: The character wants to finish homework or play a game. | Why: This is ordinary goal conflict with no commitment constraint, so it does not match.

## Boundary Stress Test
- If the scene has only a wish and a temptation, do not use this unit.
- If a promise was already canceled or explicitly made flexible, the commitment constraint may no longer decide priority.
- If the question asks what someone will likely choose because of habit or emotion, this is the wrong unit unless priority ordering is explicit.

## Generalization Note
This unit generalizes across calendars, promises, deadlines, duties, invitations, and active tasks whenever the task is to rank which commitment should take precedence.
