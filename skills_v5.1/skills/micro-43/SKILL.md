# Harm and Responsibility

## Use When
- Use when the question asks who caused harm and whether that person is blameworthy.
- Use when intent, negligence, or causal responsibility for an injury, loss, or damage is the key issue.
- Use when the task is to assign responsibility rather than judge politeness, fairness, or trust.
- Use when the scene asks whether blame is appropriate after a harmful outcome.

## Do Not Use When
- Do not use when the main issue is social tact, embarrassment, or offense without responsibility for harm.
- Do not use when the question is about fairness of outcomes or reciprocity rather than blame for harm.
- Do not use when the task is about whether someone is reliable or trustworthy over time.
- Do not use when the question is about apology, repair, or reconciliation after harm rather than responsibility itself.

## Decision Variable
blameworthy responsibility for harm

## Trigger Checklist
- Was there actual harm, damage, injury, loss, or wrongful consequence?
- Did the person cause it directly or through negligent action?
- Was the harm intentional, reckless, or accidental?
- Is the question asking who deserves blame rather than who was rude or awkward?
- Would changing the cause or intent change the answer?

## Workflow
- Identify the harmful outcome.
- Trace who caused it and whether the causation is direct or indirect.
- Check intent: intentional, reckless, negligent, or accidental.
- Assign blame only if responsibility is supported by cause plus culpability.
- Ignore pure etiquette violations unless they also caused harm.

## Special Case
**Accident versus culpable harm**: An accidental outcome is not automatically blameworthy; blame depends on whether the person could reasonably have prevented the harm or acted carelessly.

Scene: A child drops a vase while running; no one told them the area was off-limits, and they did not see it was fragile.
Question: Should the child be blamed for the broken vase?
Answer logic: The vase was broken, but the child did not intend the harm and lacked clear responsibility cues. The correct judgment is low or no blame unless there was evidence of recklessness.

## Boundary Exit Rule
- Exit to faux pas/offense if the issue is mainly social appropriateness, embarrassment, or tact with no real harm judgment.
- Exit to fairness/reciprocity if the issue is unequal treatment, repayment, or taking advantage rather than causing harm.
- Exit to trust/reputation if the question is about whether someone is generally reliable, not whether they are blameworthy for this harm.
- Exit to apology/repair if the main task is how to respond after the harm, not who caused it.
