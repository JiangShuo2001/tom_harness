# macro-12 Examples and Boundaries

## Decision Variable
whether behavior creates offense, awkwardness, or face threat

## Route Signal
The scene centers on embarrassment, offense, awkwardness, exposure, or loss of face caused by a remark, secret, or disclosure.

## Hard Boundary
- Primary task is repair or apology after harm.
- Primary task is truth evaluation, lying detection, or half-truth detection.
- Primary task is indirect speech interpretation.
- Primary task is power or hierarchy communication.
- Primary task is action selection, preference conflict, or perspective taking.

## Shortcut To Avoid
- Do not reduce the case to 'what was meant' when the question is really 'was this embarrassing or offensive?'
- Do not jump to apology logic when the prompt only asks whether the act was a faux pas.
- Do not treat every private disclosure as a lie or deception question.
- Do not overfit to one benchmark-style embarrassment item; use the reusable face-threat prototype.

## Common Failure Modes
- Confusing offense with later repair and answering with an apology strategy.
- Confusing embarrassment with literal falsehood and routing to deception.
- Missing that the offense comes from audience context, not from the content alone.
- Ignoring the target's face loss and focusing only on speaker intent.
- Overweighting politeness words while missing the social harm of disclosure.

## Minimal Pair
- Case: Someone blurts out a friend's secret in front of a group. | Why: The decisive issue is embarrassment, privacy violation, and face threat caused by the disclosure.
- Case: After making a rude comment, the speaker later apologizes and asks how to make it right. | Why: This is here only if the question is whether the original comment was offensive; if the question is about repair, it belongs elsewhere.

## Boundary Stress Test
- If the same words were said privately, would the social harm disappear?
- If no one else heard the remark, is the embarrassment still the core issue?
- If the prompt asks how to fix the situation, does the task shift out of this macro?
- If the statement is true but hurtful, does the harm come from offense rather than deception?

## Generalization Note
This macro generalizes to any scene where social harm is created by exposure, tact failure, or face threat, even when the exact surface form changes across secrets, jokes, remarks, or disclosures.
