# micro-32 Examples and Boundaries

## Decision Variable
emotional transmission from another person into the self

## Route Signal
Use this micro unit when a person's emotional state is socially transmitted to another person and the diagnostic question is how the second person's own state is affected.

## Hard Boundary
- Understanding another person's emotion is not enough; the target must be emotionally affected.
- Moral evaluation is not empathy unless the other person's emotion is the causal bridge into the target's feeling.
- Counterfactual comparison is outside this unit unless another person's emotion is what transmits to the target.
- Emotion regulation is outside this unit once the focus shifts from catching an emotion to managing it.

## Shortcut To Avoid
- Do not assume every observer feels the same emotion as the source; exposure, relationship, attention, and context matter.
- Do not answer only with the source person's feeling when the question asks about the observer's affected state.

## Common Failure Modes
- Confusing cognitive perspective-taking with emotional contagion.
- Treating sympathy as guilt or responsibility when no blame appraisal is present.
- Ignoring that the target may feel concern rather than an exact copy of the source emotion.
- Attributing the target's feeling to the original event instead of to the other person's displayed or known emotion.

## Minimal Pair
- Case: A child sees another child crying after dropping an ice cream and begins to feel sad too. | Why: The child's sadness is transmitted from the other child's visible distress.
- Case: A child sees another child crying, realizes the child is sad, and reports, 'He feels bad,' but remains cheerful. | Why: This does not match micro-32; it is emotion recognition without emotional transmission into the observer.

## Boundary Stress Test
- If the observer feels bad because they caused the other person's pain, route to moral emotion rather than empathy unless the question specifically asks about catching or sharing the pain.
- If the observer feels relieved because someone else avoided danger, check whether the relief comes from counterfactual comparison or from empathic response to the person's relief.
- If the observer stays calm while comforting someone, do not infer contagion unless the scene shows their own emotion changing.
- If a crowd becomes excited because a few people cheer, this can be contagion when the question asks about mood spreading.

## Generalization Note
This unit applies across close relationships, crowds, teams, caregiving, conflict, and online interaction whenever another person's emotion becomes input to the target's own emotional state.
