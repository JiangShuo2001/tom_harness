# Empathy and Emotional Contagion

## Use When
- Use when the question asks how one person's visible, known, or imagined emotion changes another person's own feelings.
- Use when the key inference is emotional resonance, catching a mood, shared distress, sympathetic sadness, vicarious joy, or becoming tense because someone else is tense.
- Use when the observer's feeling is caused by another person's emotional state rather than by the original event directly.
- Use when distinguishing understanding another person's emotion from being emotionally affected by it.

## Do Not Use When
- Do not use when the task only asks what the first person feels; route to the appropriate emotion appraisal unit instead.
- Do not use when the observer merely identifies or labels another person's emotion without being affected by it.
- Do not use when guilt, shame, pride, gratitude, blame, or moral responsibility is the deciding factor; use micro-30.
- Do not use when regret, relief, disappointment, or surprise depends on comparing reality with an alternative outcome; use micro-31.
- Do not use when the main issue is how someone suppresses, redirects, comforts, or manages their own emotion; use micro-33.
- Do not use when the question is only about the explicit meaning of words; use micro-34.

## Decision Variable
emotional transmission from another person into the self

## Trigger Checklist
- Is there a source person with an emotion, mood, distress, excitement, fear, sadness, or joy?
- Is there a target person who perceives, learns about, imagines, or is near that emotion?
- Does the question ask what the target person feels because of the source person's emotion?
- Would the target person's state change if the source person's emotion were absent?
- Is the answer about resonance, sympathy, contagion, or vicarious feeling rather than moral blame, counterfactual comparison, or self-regulation?

## Workflow
- Identify the source person's emotional state and what evidence makes it available to the target.
- Check whether the target notices, understands, or is exposed to that state strongly enough for affective transfer.
- Infer the likely direction of transmission: matching emotion, softened sympathetic concern, shared excitement, anxiety spread, or emotional dampening.
- Separate emotional transmission from the target's own direct appraisal of the event.
- Answer in terms of the target's resulting feeling and the causal link to the other person's emotion.

## Special Case
**Sympathy without identical emotion**: Empathy does not always copy the source emotion exactly; the target may feel concern, sadness, tenderness, or anxiety in response to another person's pain or fear.

Scene: Maya sees her friend trembling before a speech. Maya is not giving a speech, but she starts to feel tense and worried for her friend.
Question: Why does Maya feel tense?
Answer logic: Maya's tension is caused by perceiving her friend's fear. The relevant variable is emotional transmission into Maya, not Maya's own direct risk or responsibility.

## Boundary Exit Rule
- Exit to micro-30 if the target's emotion depends mainly on judging who caused harm, who deserves blame, or whether a norm was violated.
- Exit to micro-31 if the target's emotion depends mainly on comparing what happened with what could have happened.
- Exit to micro-33 if the target already has an emotion and the question asks how they manage or change it.
- Exit to micro-34 if the only required answer is what an utterance literally says.
- Exit to another emotion unit if the target's feeling is caused directly by their own goal success, loss, threat, or appraisal rather than by another person's emotion.
