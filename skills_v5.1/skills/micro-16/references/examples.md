# micro-16 Examples and Boundaries

## Decision Variable
whether the fact is mutually known as public shared knowledge

## Route Signal
Use when the scene asks whether a fact is publicly shared and mutually known by both parties.

## Hard Boundary
- Not for private belief, hidden belief, or guessing.
- Not for predicting behavior from belief.
- Not for credibility, rumor, or update from new evidence.
- Not for appearance-reality contrasts.

## Shortcut To Avoid
- Do not assume common knowledge just because both people heard the same words once.
- Do not convert shared information into second-order prediction unless the question explicitly asks what one person thinks about the other's belief.

## Common Failure Modes
- Confusing mutual knowledge with second-order belief.
- Overcalling common knowledge from a public event without explicit mutual awareness.
- Answering with who is right instead of who knows what.
- Using source reliability when the task is only about sharedness.

## Minimal Pair
- Case: A teacher tells the whole class the test is tomorrow, and everyone heard it. | Why: The question is about whether the information is openly shared by all and mutually available.
- Case: A child privately tells one friend a secret, and the other friend does not hear it. | Why: This is not common knowledge because the fact is not mutually known.

## Boundary Stress Test
- Publicly announced, but one person was distracted and missed it: do not call this common knowledge.
- Both people know the fact, but the question asks what one expects the other to do: route to second-order belief instead.

## Generalization Note
This unit tracks public sharedness of information, not belief depth, not source quality, and not memory of how the fact was learned.
