# macro-15 Examples and Boundaries

## Decision Variable
How long-term history affects trust, reputation, identity, role, and relationship judgment.

## Route Signal
Long-term history, reputation, loyalty, trustworthiness, role, or identity determines the answer.

## Hard Boundary
- Single-source credibility is not enough by itself.
- A one-off lie or one-off mistake does not automatically make this a relationship-history case.
- If the scene is mainly about apology or damage repair, it belongs outside this macro.
- If the scene is mainly about live clarification of misunderstanding, it belongs outside this macro.

## Shortcut To Avoid
- Do not answer from the most recent event alone.
- Do not treat trust as a pure truth-checking problem.
- Do not ignore the established relationship baseline.
- Do not collapse role, reputation, and trust into generic belief reasoning.

## Common Failure Modes
- Overweighting a single new action and forgetting the track record.
- Confusing reputation judgment with literal truth assessment.
- Routing apology or reconciliation scenes here when repair is the real target.
- Treating role-based trust as mere familiarity rather than a long-term pattern.

## Minimal Pair
- Case: A manager must decide whether to assign a sensitive task to an employee who has been reliable for years. | Why: The answer depends on long-term trust and reputation, not a one-off statement.
- Case: A friend asks whether to forgive someone after repeated broken promises over many months. | Why: The key variable is relational history and its effect on trust and future interaction.

## Boundary Stress Test
- If the scene gives only one statement from a stranger, this macro should usually not be used.
- If the scene is about whether to apologize, compensate, or reconcile after offense, leave this macro.
- If the scene asks what someone means by a remark, do not solve it here unless trust history is the main reason.

## Generalization Note
This macro generalizes to any scene where accumulated interaction history changes trust, reputation, role expectations, or relationship judgment, even when the wording differs from benchmark-style prompts.
