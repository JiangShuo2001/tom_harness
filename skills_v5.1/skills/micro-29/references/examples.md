# micro-29 Examples and Boundaries

## Decision Variable
the social display rule governing expression

## Route Signal
Use this micro unit when a character's outward emotion is strategically shaped by a display rule tied to politeness, role, audience, or situation.

## Hard Boundary
- This unit is about rule-governed expression control, not all hidden emotion.
- The decisive evidence must include a display norm or social expectation.
- The answer should explain both the inner feeling and why a different outward expression is socially required or useful.
- Do not infer deception automatically; many display rules are polite, protective, professional, or ceremonial rather than dishonest.

## Shortcut To Avoid
- Do not assume the displayed emotion is the true emotion just because it is visible.
- Do not label every smile as happiness; check whether the social setting rewards smiling.
- Do not route to hidden emotion only when the question asks why the person manages expression.
- Do not over-moralize politeness masking as lying unless truthfulness or intent to mislead is the main issue.

## Common Failure Modes
- Ignoring the audience and treating the expression as purely personal.
- Explaining the inner feeling but omitting the display rule.
- Confusing emotional disguise with atypical emotion.
- Confusing professional calm or ceremonial composure with lack of emotion.
- Treating culturally or role-specific display expectations as universal without using scene evidence.

## Minimal Pair
- Case: A child dislikes a birthday gift but smiles because the giver is watching. | Why: The outward smile is governed by a politeness display rule for receiving gifts.
- Case: A child dislikes a birthday gift and secretly feels sad after the giver leaves. | Why: This does not by itself match unless the task asks about controlled outward expression; otherwise it is only internal emotion or hidden emotion.

## Boundary Stress Test
- If a nurse stays calm while frightened during an emergency, check whether professional role expectations explain the calm display.
- If a contestant smiles after losing, check whether sportsmanship or public-facing expectations govern the expression rather than genuine happiness.
- If someone laughs at bad news because they are confused or have an unusual appraisal, route away to atypical emotion unless a display rule explains the laugh.
- If someone apologizes and feels guilt, route to moral emotion when responsibility appraisal is the central variable, even if they also controls their face.

## Generalization Note
Display rules vary by culture, role, relationship, and situation, but the operational pattern is stable: infer the felt emotion, identify the social expectation, and explain the controlled outward display.
