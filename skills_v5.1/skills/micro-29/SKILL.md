# Emotional Disguise and Display Rules

## Use When
- Use when the question asks why a character smiles, stays calm, hides disappointment, acts pleased, or otherwise manages emotional expression for politeness, professionalism, safety, ceremony, or role expectations.
- Use when the key issue is not merely what the person feels, but what social rule determines what they should show.
- Use when outward emotion is shaped by audience, setting, relationship, status, or occasion.
- Use when explaining a deliberate or learned expression-control strategy such as masking, softening, exaggerating, neutralizing, or substituting an emotion.

## Do Not Use When
- Do not use when the task only asks to detect that the true emotion differs from the displayed emotion without asking why the display is socially controlled; use micro-28 instead.
- Do not use when the emotion itself is surprising or nonstandard, but there is no rule-based expression management; use micro-27 instead.
- Do not use when guilt, shame, pride, gratitude, blame, or moral responsibility is the central cause of the emotion; use micro-30 instead.
- Do not use when regret, relief, disappointment, or surprise depends on comparing the real outcome with an alternative outcome; use micro-31 instead.
- Do not use when the display is accidental leakage, a spontaneous facial cue, or a physiological reaction rather than controlled expression.

## Decision Variable
the social display rule governing expression

## Trigger Checklist
- Is there a mismatch, adjustment, or filtering between felt emotion and shown emotion?
- Is the adjustment motivated by a social rule, role, audience, occasion, politeness norm, or expectation?
- Can the character identify who is present and what expression would be acceptable or unacceptable there?
- Does the answer require explaining the outward display rather than only naming the inner feeling?
- Would the display change if the audience, setting, or role changed?

## Workflow
- Identify the character's likely internal emotion from events, goals, and appraisal.
- Identify the audience, relationship, setting, role, or occasion that creates a display expectation.
- State the relevant display rule, such as being polite to a host, staying calm as a leader, not gloating in front of a loser, or smiling for a photo.
- Compare the true feeling with the socially acceptable expression.
- Explain the chosen expression-control tactic: hide, soften, exaggerate, replace, delay, or neutralize the emotion.
- Answer in terms of rule-governed outward display, not just internal emotion.

## Special Case
**Polite happiness despite disappointment**: When someone receives a gift, compliment, invitation, or public recognition, politeness may require showing appreciation even if the true feeling is disappointment or discomfort.

Scene: Maya hoped for a new sketchbook, but her aunt gives her a sweater she does not like. Her aunt is watching her face.
Question: Why does Maya smile and say she likes it?
Answer logic: Maya may feel disappointed, but the display rule for receiving a gift is to show gratitude and avoid hurting the giver's feelings, so she masks disappointment with a polite smile.

## Boundary Exit Rule
- If the question can be answered by naming the hidden true emotion without explaining a social rule for the outward expression, exit to micro-28.
- If the central puzzle is why the true emotion is unusual for the situation, exit to micro-27.
- If the central appraisal is responsibility, wrongdoing, deservedness, or norm violation, exit to micro-30.
- If the emotion depends on 'what might have happened instead,' exit to micro-31.
- If the expression is primarily a communicative signal such as a wink, glance, or gesture meant to convey an intention, route to the relevant communication or nonverbal-intention unit instead.
