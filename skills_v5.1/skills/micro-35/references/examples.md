# micro-35 Examples and Boundaries

## Decision Variable
the social action the utterance is performing

## Route Signal
Use this micro unit when an utterance must be classified by its social function in context, especially for indirect requests, refusals, reminders, warnings, hints, or invitations.

## Hard Boundary
- Literal Meaning asks what is said, not what act is done.
- Conversational Implicature asks what extra meaning is conveyed, not which speech act is being performed.
- Truth, Lie, and Half-Truth asks whether the content matches facts, not what the utterance is doing socially.

## Shortcut To Avoid
- Do not answer with the literal paraphrase if the utterance is clearly serving another social function.
- Do not over-infer hidden motives when the task only asks for the speech act.
- Do not confuse a polite form with a different act if context makes the intended act obvious.

## Common Failure Modes
- Answering the literal semantic content instead of the speech act.
- Labeling every indirect utterance as implicature even when the target is the action type.
- Treating a warning or reminder as a statement of fact only.
- Missing that a question can function as a request.

## Minimal Pair
- Case: A says, 'Can you pass the salt?' at dinner. | Why: The utterance is a question in form but functions socially as a request.
- Case: A says, 'You left your keys on the table.' to B who is walking away. | Why: The utterance may function as a reminder or alert, so the key variable is the social act performed.

## Boundary Stress Test
- If the same words are spoken in a different context, does the social act change?
- Can you name the act without judging truth, motive, or implied extra content?
- If the utterance is stripped of context, do you still need context to identify the act rather than the literal meaning?

## Generalization Note
This unit generalizes to any case where form and function diverge and the task is to classify the communicative act, not the proposition itself.
