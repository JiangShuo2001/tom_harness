# micro-22 Examples and Boundaries

## Decision Variable
the immediate purpose behind the behavior

## Route Signal
Use this micro unit when an observable behavior is the evidence and the diagnostic question is what purpose that behavior serves right now.

## Hard Boundary
- This unit explains the current behavior; it does not forecast the next behavior.
- This unit identifies an immediate intention; it does not resolve deep personality traits, stable preferences, or long-term values.
- This unit may use beliefs and goals as evidence, but the output is not a belief state or a goal hierarchy.
- This unit applies only when the behavior is plausibly intentional or socially meaningful.

## Shortcut To Avoid
- Do not infer the actor's intention from what the observer knows if the actor lacks that information.
- Do not jump from a cue directly to a future action; first answer what the cue is trying to accomplish now.
- Do not treat every smile, glance, or pause as deception or romance; use scene constraints and audience effects.

## Common Failure Modes
- Answering with a predicted action instead of the current purpose.
- Explaining the behavior from the narrator's omniscient knowledge rather than the actor's perspective.
- Ignoring the target of the cue, such as who the gaze, gesture, or signal is directed toward.
- Choosing a broad motive like 'to be nice' when a more specific immediate purpose is supported.
- Confusing stable preference with momentary intention.

## Minimal Pair
- Case: Sam points at the spilled water while looking at the waiter. The question asks why Sam points. | Why: The answer is the immediate purpose of a current gesture: to draw the waiter's attention to the spill or request cleanup.
- Case: Sam points at the spilled water, then the question asks what the waiter will do next. | Why: This is a near neighbor but routes to action prediction because the requested answer is the waiter's likely next behavior, not Sam's current intention.

## Boundary Stress Test
- If a person glances at the door during a meeting, decide whether the glance is checking the time, signaling a desire to leave, noticing someone enter, or avoiding eye contact based on timing and audience.
- If a child hides a broken toy behind their back, separate the immediate intention to conceal the damage from the later action they may take.
- If a speaker pauses before answering a sensitive question, test whether the pause is for thinking, politeness, concealment, or emotional regulation using context rather than stereotype.
- If someone waves across a room, identify whether the wave is greeting, summoning, warning, or confirming attention before predicting any follow-up.

## Generalization Note
The unit generalizes across verbal and nonverbal behavior whenever the same inference pattern is used: observable cue plus actor perspective plus context yields an immediate social purpose.
