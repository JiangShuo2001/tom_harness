# micro-34 Examples and Boundaries

## Decision Variable
explicit semantic content

## Route Signal
Use this micro unit when the diagnostic target is the utterance's explicitly stated meaning, independent of hidden intention or pragmatic effect.

## Hard Boundary
- Literal Meaning stops at what the words conventionally encode.
- It does not infer what the speaker wants the listener to do.
- It does not infer what the speaker privately believes or feels.
- It does not infer unstated conclusions from conversational norms unless the question asks only for explicit content.
- It does not judge whether the utterance is socially appropriate, persuasive, deceptive, or emotionally comforting.

## Shortcut To Avoid
- Do not automatically replace a polite question with the likely request it performs.
- Do not treat the most socially plausible interpretation as the literal meaning.
- Do not add unstated causal, emotional, or moral conclusions just because they are likely in the scene.

## Common Failure Modes
- Answering the implied request instead of the literal question.
- Confusing explicit denial with hidden reluctance.
- Treating sarcasm, politeness, or tact as part of the literal sentence content.
- Using the speaker's likely motive to rewrite what the sentence says.
- Overusing context to infer more than reference resolution.

## Minimal Pair
- Case: A sign says, 'The museum closes at 6 p.m.' The question asks what the sign says. | Why: The answer is the explicit posted content: the museum closes at 6 p.m.
- Case: A guard points to the sign and says, 'The museum closes at 6 p.m.' while visitors are still inside. The question asks what the guard is trying to get them to do. | Why: This is a near neighbor, not this unit: the explicit content is about closing time, but the requested answer concerns an indirect warning or request to leave.

## Boundary Stress Test
- If an utterance is literally false but politely useful, keep literal content separate from social function.
- If an utterance is literally a question but conventionally used as a request, answer only the question form unless asked for intention.
- If context makes a hidden meaning obvious, do not include it in the literal paraphrase.
- If a word is ambiguous, use stated context only to choose its reference or sense; do not infer a broader unstated message.

## Generalization Note
Across benchmark and real-world cases, this unit is the semantic baseline: first state what the utterance explicitly encodes, then route elsewhere if the task requires pragmatic, emotional, deceptive, or relational interpretation.
