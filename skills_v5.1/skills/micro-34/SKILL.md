# Literal Meaning

## Use When
- Use when the question asks what a sentence, phrase, sign, message, or spoken utterance explicitly says.
- Use when the answer can be found by parsing the actual words and their conventional meanings.
- Use when the task is to distinguish the stated content from what the speaker may have intended, implied, hinted, or caused the listener to feel.
- Use when checking whether a statement is literally compatible with a fact, instruction, label, or description.

## Do Not Use When
- Do not use when the main question is what social action the utterance performs, such as requesting, warning, refusing, reminding, or inviting; route to Indirect Speech Act.
- Do not use when the main question is what extra meaning is communicated by context but not said; route to Conversational Implicature.
- Do not use when the main question is whether the speaker is lying, concealing, persuading, or strategically wording the message.
- Do not use when the main question is how the listener feels, how the speaker regulates emotion, or how emotion spreads between people.
- Do not use if the answer requires adding unstated motives, assumptions about politeness, audience reaction, or relationship history.

## Decision Variable
explicit semantic content

## Trigger Checklist
- Is there a quoted or clearly identifiable utterance?
- Is the question asking what the words say rather than why they were said?
- Can the answer be produced from grammar, word meaning, reference, and stated facts alone?
- Would the answer remain the same even if the speaker had a different hidden motive?
- Are contextual facts needed only to resolve reference, such as who 'she' or 'that box' refers to, not to infer a hidden message?

## Workflow
- Identify the exact utterance or written message to interpret.
- Resolve ordinary references using only stated context, such as names, pronouns, time, place, and objects.
- Paraphrase the utterance in direct, explicit terms without adding motives or social functions.
- Check whether the paraphrase includes only what is semantically asserted, asked, commanded, or described.
- If the question asks what the speaker is doing socially or what is implied beyond the words, stop and route out.

## Special Case
**Literal question form versus intended request**: When an utterance is phrased as a question, first report the literal question it asks; do not convert it into a request unless the task asks for the social action.

Scene: During dinner, Alex says, 'Can you pass the salt?'
Question: What does Alex's sentence literally ask?
Answer logic: Literally, the sentence asks whether the listener is able to pass the salt. Interpreting it as a request to pass the salt belongs to Indirect Speech Act, not Literal Meaning.

## Boundary Exit Rule
- If the target answer uses words like 'means that she wants,' 'is hinting,' 'is asking indirectly,' 'is implying,' or 'really means,' exit to a pragmatic unit.
- If the answer depends on the listener's private knowledge, relationship history, politeness norms, or likely reaction, exit this unit unless those facts are only resolving a reference.
- If the issue is truthfulness, deception, omission, or misleading wording, exit to a lie, half-truth, or misdirection unit.
- If the issue is emotional impact or emotion management rather than sentence content, exit to the relevant emotion unit.
