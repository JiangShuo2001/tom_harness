# macro-20 Examples and Boundaries

## Decision Variable
Which communication action is most appropriate given the power gap and the goal of the exchange.

## Route Signal
A status difference changes what kind of message, tone, or channel is appropriate, and the answer is about how to communicate across that gap.

## Hard Boundary
- Not a negotiation problem when only one side can realistically decide the message.
- Not a pure self-presentation problem when the main choice is how to address a higher or lower status person.
- Not a pure role-identification problem when the role is known but the communication move is still undecided.

## Shortcut To Avoid
- Do not default to the most polite answer if the real need is a clear escalation.
- Do not treat hierarchy as decorative context; it can determine the correct action.
- Do not collapse upward communication, downward communication, and peer communication into one generic politeness rule.

## Common Failure Modes
- Answering with content instead of the communication move.
- Choosing deference when the situation requires a respectful but explicit challenge.
- Choosing a direct challenge when the context requires formal escalation.
- Confusing reputation management with power-gap communication.

## Minimal Pair
- Case: A student asks a teacher whether they can resubmit homework after missing the deadline. | Why: The correct response depends on how to request something across a status gap.
- Case: Two coworkers of equal rank disagree on a project plan and need a compromise. | Why: This is peer-level conflict and belongs to negotiation, not power-gap communication.

## Boundary Stress Test
- A subordinate knows the boss is wrong and must decide whether to say so in private, in public, or by email.
- A customer wants to complain to a manager rather than to a front-line worker.
- A junior doctor must decide whether to question a senior doctor's instruction immediately or escalate after the shift.
- A child must decide how to ask a parent for permission after being told no.

## Generalization Note
This prototype generalizes across workplace, family, school, service, legal, and institutional settings whenever hierarchy changes what communication action is appropriate.
