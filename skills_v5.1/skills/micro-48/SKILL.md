# Privacy, Secrets, and Boundaries

## Use When
- Use when the task is to decide what information may be shared and what should remain private.
- Use when the main variable is the disclosure boundary itself: secret, private fact, confidential detail, or off-limits topic.
- Use when the question is about whether telling someone is allowed, appropriate, or too revealing.

## Do Not Use When
- Do not use when the main issue is repairing harm after the information was already revealed; use relationship repair instead.
- Do not use when the main issue is self-presentation, image management, or choosing how to present oneself; use impression management instead.
- Do not use when the main issue is role, status, identity group, or hierarchy rather than disclosure.

## Decision Variable
whether the information is within the disclosure boundary

## Trigger Checklist
- Is the content a secret, private fact, or confidential detail?
- Is the question asking whether it should be told, withheld, or kept within a defined audience?
- Has the scene defined a boundary such as personal, family, professional, or relational confidentiality?
- Is the key judgment about disclosure permission rather than apology, persuasion, or role?

## Workflow
- Identify the item of information that might be shared.
- Ask who is entitled to know it under the scene's boundary.
- Check whether the information is private by default, explicitly shared, or already public.
- If disclosure would cross the boundary, keep it private; if it is within the allowed circle, it may be shared.

## Special Case
**Already Shared Means Exit to Repair**: If the information has already been revealed, this unit no longer applies; switch to repair, apology, or relationship recovery.

Scene: A friend accidentally tells another person a private diagnosis that was meant to stay secret.
Question: Should this unit decide what to do next?
Answer logic: No. The boundary has already been crossed, so the active question is repair after disclosure, not whether the information may be shared.

## Boundary Exit Rule
- Exit if the question is about how to apologize, explain, or restore trust after disclosure.
- Exit if the question is about how to present oneself or control impressions rather than protect information.
- Exit if the issue is role-based access, group identity, or power hierarchy rather than privacy.
- Exit if the scene is mainly about lying, persuasion, or indirect meaning rather than confidentiality.
