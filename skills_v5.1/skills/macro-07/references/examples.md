# macro-07 Examples and Boundaries

## Decision Variable
what the speaker really wants the listener to understand

## Route Signal
A speaker says one thing but clearly intends the listener to infer a different social action or practical meaning.

## Hard Boundary
- Not about whether the utterance is factually true.
- Not about hidden feeling unless the feeling is only a clue to the intended message.
- Not about optimizing persuasion strategy or audience calibration.
- Not about nested belief or false-belief reasoning unless needed only as context.

## Shortcut To Avoid
- Do not overcall this macro for any nonliteral sentence; some nonliteral cases are lies, sarcasm with truth-status questions, or persuasion problems.
- Do not answer from literal semantics alone when the scene clearly signals an intended social meaning.
- Do not confuse indirectness with emotional concealment.

## Common Failure Modes
- Answering the literal sentence instead of the intended meaning.
- Routing sarcasm or irony to emotion when the task is really conversational meaning.
- Routing a deceptive statement here when the core is truth value or misleading effect.
- Treating a polite request as a factual comment.

## Minimal Pair
- Case: A person says, 'Can you pass the salt?' at dinner. | Why: The literal form is a question about ability, but the intended meaning is a request.
- Case: A person says, 'Nice job,' after someone spills a drink and is obviously annoyed. | Why: The literal words are positive, but the intended meaning is criticism or irritation.

## Boundary Stress Test
- If the utterance is indirect but the question asks whether it is true, shift to lies or misdirection.
- If the utterance is indirect but the main challenge is tailoring it to the listener, shift to persuasion or advice.
- If the utterance hides feeling without changing the social act, shift to hidden emotion.
- If the utterance is a live misunderstanding that needs repair, this macro can still be used for the meaning inference, but the repair macro may become the better direct route.

## Generalization Note
Use this macro as the reusable prototype for indirect speech acts across requests, warnings, complaints, refusals, reminders, invitations, and soft criticism; micro ids are only for diagnosis, repair, and boundary explanation.
