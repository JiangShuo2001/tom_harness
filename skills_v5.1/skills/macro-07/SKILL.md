# Indirect Language and Conversational Meaning

## Use When
- The speaker means something beyond the literal sentence, and the task is to infer the intended social meaning.
- The utterance functions as a request, warning, complaint, refusal, reminder, invitation, or soft criticism rather than plain factual description.
- The key question is what the listener is supposed to understand from the way it was said.

## Do Not Use When
- Do not use when the main issue is whether the statement is true or deceptive; route to lies, half-truths, or misdirection instead.
- Do not use when the main issue is the speaker's hidden emotion rather than the communicative intent.
- Do not use when the core task is audience adaptation, persuasion strategy, or choosing the best way to influence a listener.
- Do not use when the scene is a straightforward literal statement with no implied social meaning.

## Decision Variable
what the speaker really wants the listener to understand

## Direct Route Rule
Route immediately when spoken words mean a request, warning, complaint, refusal, reminder, or invitation beyond literal text.

## Trigger Checklist
- The literal words are incomplete, softened, or socially indirect.
- The scene asks for the intended meaning, not the literal sentence meaning.
- A request, warning, complaint, refusal, reminder, or invitation is being conveyed indirectly.
- The listener must infer what is meant from context, tone, or shared expectations.
- The utterance is not primarily about whether it is true or false.

## Workflow
- Identify the literal utterance.
- Ask what social act the speaker is performing through that utterance.
- Use context, relationship, and situational need to infer the intended meaning.
- Prefer the indirect communicative function over the literal wording.
- State the intended message in plain language.

## Special Case
**Polite indirect request**: If a question or statement is phrased politely but clearly functions as a request, answer with the requested action or the intended request, not the literal sentence meaning.

Scene: A guest says, 'It is quite warm in here,' while looking at the closed window.
Question: What does the guest really want the host to understand?
Answer logic: The literal sentence is about temperature, but the social meaning is a polite request to open the window or cool the room.

## Boundary Exit Rule
- Exit to macro-08 if the central issue is truthfulness, deception, or selective disclosure.
- Exit to macro-05 if the central issue is emotion inference from expression rather than communicative intent.
- Exit to macro-09 if the central issue is how to persuade, advise, or adapt to the listener.
- Exit to macro-04 if the carrier of meaning is a nonverbal cue rather than spoken words.
- Add micro-34 when the literal meaning must be pinned down before the indirect meaning is read.
- Add micro-35 when the utterance functions as a request, warning, refusal, or invitation in disguise.
- Add micro-36 when context-driven implicature must be computed.
- Add micro-39 when the puzzle is why the speaker phrased it indirectly (motive).
- Add micro-48 when the indirect wording protects a private or sensitive disclosure.
