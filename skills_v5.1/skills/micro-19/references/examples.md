# micro-19 Examples and Boundaries

## Decision Variable
the character's own preference

## Route Signal
Route here when the diagnostic question is the character's own liking, wanting, or choice preference.

## Hard Boundary
- Another person's preference belongs to Other Preference, not Self Preference.
- Uncertainty about a preference belongs to Uncertain Belief or Source Credibility.
- Goal ordering and action selection belong to Goal Hierarchy or action-related units, not Self Preference.

## Shortcut To Avoid
- Do not treat a polite statement as the true preference unless the scene clearly indicates it reflects genuine liking.
- Do not answer from what would be useful or expected; answer from what the character themselves wants.

## Common Failure Modes
- Confusing the character's own preference with their guess about someone else's preference.
- Using a goal or practical constraint as if it were a preference.
- Overreading social politeness as hidden desire without evidence.

## Minimal Pair
- Case: A character is offered apples and oranges and picks apples every time. | Why: The question is about that character's own stable liking.
- Case: A character says, 'I think my friend wants apples more than oranges.' | Why: This is not self preference; it is a representation of another person's preference.

## Boundary Stress Test
- If the scene asks, 'What does she think he likes?', do not use this unit.
- If the scene asks, 'What does she want right now?' and the answer is based on her own desire, use this unit.
- If the scene asks, 'Which option will she pick because it is required?', do not use this unit unless the required option is also clearly her preference.

## Generalization Note
This unit generalizes to any stable or immediate self-directed liking, wanting, or choice preference, across food, objects, activities, partners, plans, and options.
