# micro-30 Examples and Boundaries

## Decision Variable
the responsibility or norm appraisal behind the emotion

## Route Signal
Route here when emotion inference depends on moral responsibility or norm evaluation rather than on simple event-based affect.

## Hard Boundary
- No moral appraisal, no use.
- If the scene only asks what the person feels but not why in moral terms, route elsewhere.
- If the main clue is hiding, masking, or social display, this is not the right unit.

## Shortcut To Avoid
- Do not infer guilt whenever someone looks sad.
- Do not infer pride whenever someone succeeds.
- Do not confuse anger at inconvenience with anger at wrongdoing.

## Common Failure Modes
- Replacing moral appraisal with generic negative emotion.
- Mistaking shame for embarrassment when no norm violation is judged.
- Choosing gratitude from politeness instead of from perceived benefit or help.
- Answering with outcome-based emotion when the scene is actually about blame or credit.

## Minimal Pair
- Case: A person apologizes after breaking a promise and says they feel terrible because it was their fault. | Why: The emotion is driven by self-responsibility and norm violation, which points to guilt or shame.
- Case: A person smiles after being thanked for helping and feels good because they did something valuable. | Why: The emotion is driven by credit and moral worth, which points to pride or gratitude depending on who benefited.

## Boundary Stress Test
- If the same event happened but no one was blamed or credited, would the answer change?
- If the person felt the same way but had no moral reason for it, would this still fit?
- If the question asks only whether the person is upset, this unit is too specific.

## Generalization Note
This unit generalizes across scenes where moral appraisal shapes emotion, including blame, credit, duty, fairness, harm, apology, and appreciation, without depending on any benchmark-specific story.
