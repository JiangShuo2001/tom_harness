# micro-27 Examples and Boundaries

## Decision Variable
the character-specific appraisal that reverses or alters the expected emotion

## Route Signal
Use this micro unit when the target is a genuine but nonstandard emotion and the answer depends on the character's idiosyncratic appraisal rather than on concealment or display management.

## Hard Boundary
- Atypical Emotion is about unusual internal appraisal, not misleading external expression.
- It does not handle polite smiling, forced laughter, professional calm, or strategic emotional performance unless the true felt emotion itself is also unusual.
- It does not replace basic emotion inference when the emotion is the standard result of goal success, loss, threat, or relief.

## Shortcut To Avoid
- Do not answer with the socially typical emotion if the character's stated goals, values, or history point to a different appraisal.
- Do not assume a smile or calm behavior means the person is hiding sadness; first decide whether the positive or calm emotion is genuinely felt.

## Common Failure Modes
- Treating atypical emotion as hidden emotion and inventing a concealed standard feeling.
- Ignoring a character-specific goal that reverses the emotional meaning of the event.
- Explaining only the event's objective outcome rather than how the character appraises it.
- Overgeneralizing from what most people would feel and missing personal values, past experiences, or comparison points.

## Minimal Pair
- Case: A student laughs after receiving a low grade because she expected to fail the course but the grade is high enough to pass. | Why: The positive emotion is genuinely felt and becomes coherent because her comparison point is avoiding failure, not achieving a high score.
- Case: A student smiles after receiving a low grade because the teacher is watching, but later cries alone. | Why: This does not match Atypical Emotion; the true feeling is ordinary disappointment while the smile is a managed display, so route to Hidden Emotion or Display Rules.

## Boundary Stress Test
- If someone is calm during danger because they are trained to appear calm but feel afraid, do not use this unit.
- If someone is calm during danger because they accurately believes the danger is harmless, resolve belief or knowledge first; then use basic appraisal if the calmness is ordinary under that belief.
- If someone is proud of being criticized because criticism from a respected expert signals they are being taken seriously, this unit applies.
- If someone is pleased by another person's success because they love that person, the emotion may be ordinary relational joy unless the scene emphasizes that the expected emotion was envy or resentment.

## Generalization Note
Atypical emotions become predictable when the model uses the character's own appraisal frame rather than the observer's default emotional script.
