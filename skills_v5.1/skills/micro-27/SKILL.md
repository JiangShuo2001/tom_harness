# Atypical Emotion

## Use When
- Use when the character's actual emotion is surprising, counter-normative, or not the emotion most people would predict from the event.
- Use when the question asks why someone feels glad, relieved, proud, amused, calm, sad, or angry in a situation where the ordinary expectation points elsewhere.
- Use when the key task is to identify the character-specific appraisal that makes the unusual emotion make sense.
- Use when the emotion is genuinely felt, not merely performed or concealed.

## Do Not Use When
- Do not use when the emotion follows ordinary success, failure, threat, loss, or gain; route to Basic Emotion Appraisal instead.
- Do not use when the person feels one thing internally but shows another externally; route to Hidden Emotion.
- Do not use when the main issue is a social rule for managing expression, such as politeness, professionalism, or saving face; route to Emotional Disguise and Display Rules.
- Do not use when the answer depends mainly on choosing among competing goals or actions rather than explaining an unusual feeling.
- Do not use when the emotion is unusual only to the observer because the observer lacks factual context; first resolve the character's belief or knowledge boundary.

## Decision Variable
the character-specific appraisal that reverses or alters the expected emotion

## Trigger Checklist
- An event has an obvious ordinary emotional prediction.
- The character's actual emotion differs from that ordinary prediction.
- The character is not merely pretending, masking, or following a display rule.
- There is evidence for a special value, desire, belief, past experience, identity concern, or social meaning.
- The question asks why the unusual feeling occurs, not just what the face or behavior displays.

## Workflow
- State the ordinary expected emotion for the event.
- Identify the character's actual or implied emotion.
- Find the appraisal feature that differs for this character: goal relevance, personal value, belief, memory, comparison point, relationship meaning, or identity meaning.
- Explain how that appraisal makes the unusual emotion coherent from the character's perspective.
- Check that the explanation concerns the true feeling itself, not a hidden feeling or managed display.

## Special Case
**Positive emotion after an apparently negative event**: If a person feels happy, relieved, proud, or amused after an event that normally causes sadness, fear, or embarrassment, explain the hidden benefit, avoided worse outcome, personal value, or preferred alternative that changes the appraisal.

Scene: Maya is not chosen for a weekend work trip. Most people on her team would be disappointed, but Maya smiles and says she is glad because it means she can attend her sister's concert.
Question: Why is Maya happy instead of disappointed?
Answer logic: The ordinary expectation is disappointment at missing a work opportunity. Maya's appraisal is different because staying home serves a more important personal goal. Her happiness is genuine and atypical because the event is good relative to her own priorities.

## Boundary Exit Rule
- If the main contrast is internal feeling versus outward expression, exit to micro-28 Hidden Emotion.
- If the main explanation is a norm about what emotion should be shown in public, exit to micro-29 Emotional Disguise and Display Rules.
- If the emotion is ordinary once the success, failure, threat, loss, or gain is identified, exit to micro-26 Basic Emotion Appraisal.
- If the question asks what action the person chooses among competing motives, exit to micro-25 Goal-Conflict Arbitration.
- If the unusual answer depends on what the character knows or falsely believes, resolve that belief unit before using this emotion unit.
