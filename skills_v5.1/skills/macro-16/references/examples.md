# macro-16 Examples and Boundaries

## Decision Variable
how to detect and clarify a live misunderstanding in conversation

## Route Signal
The conversation contains an immediate sign of confusion, correction, or repair: 'What do you mean?', 'No, I meant...', 'I thought you said...', or a direct restatement to align meaning.

## Hard Boundary
- Real-time clarification only, not aftermath repair.
- No primary focus on apology, compensation, or reconciliation.
- No primary focus on persuasion or negotiation.
- No primary focus on deception detection.

## Shortcut To Avoid
- Do not treat every correction as an insult.
- Do not convert a simple misunderstanding into a relationship rupture.
- Do not answer the literal content without resolving the mismatch in meaning.
- Do not jump to trust or morality unless the scene explicitly requires it.

## Common Failure Modes
- Overcalling offense when the scene is only confused communication.
- Missing the intended meaning because the listener fixates on literal wording.
- Answering the underlying topic instead of the repair move.
- Using apology logic where clarification logic is enough.

## Minimal Pair
- Case: A person says, 'I need the report by Friday,' and the listener asks, 'Do you mean this Friday or next Friday?' | Why: This is a live misunderstanding that can be fixed immediately by clarifying the intended referent.
- Case: A person says something that embarrasses or hurts the other, who later asks for an apology and repair. | Why: This is not just misunderstanding; the main issue is relationship damage and belongs outside this macro.

## Boundary Stress Test
- The wording is identical, but one version is only ambiguous and another version has caused offense; route only the ambiguous version here.
- The listener already understood correctly but asks for rhetorical effect; do not overroute.
- A third party is confused, but the original speaker and listener already share meaning; do not overroute unless the live repair is central.

## Generalization Note
This macro covers any in-the-moment conversational repair where the task is to recover shared meaning from ambiguity, mishearing, or wrong inference, using the lightest effective clarification move.
