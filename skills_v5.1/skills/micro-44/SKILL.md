# Fairness and Reciprocity

## Use When
- Use when the main question is whether resources, burdens, favors, or obligations are fairly balanced.
- Use when the scene asks if someone is taking advantage, freeloading, overpaying, or not repaying a favor.
- Use when the answer depends on reciprocity rather than on making a new deal or settling conflict.

## Do Not Use When
- Do not use when the task is to design or negotiate a better deal; that is negotiation, not fairness judgment.
- Do not use when the issue is blame for harm, apology, or fault; that is responsibility or repair.
- Do not use when the issue is trustworthiness over time, role hierarchy, or social tact; those are separate units.
- Do not use when the key question is simply whether someone should feel embarrassed or offended.

## Decision Variable
whether the exchange or obligation is balanced and reciprocal

## Trigger Checklist
- Is someone giving more, paying more, or carrying more than the other side?
- Is there an expected return favor, repayment, or shared burden?
- Does the scene hinge on equal treatment, proportional payoff, or unfair advantage?
- Is the main question about whether the imbalance is justified or exploitative?

## Workflow
- Identify the two sides of the exchange: what each person gave, owes, or received.
- Compare the exchange for balance, reciprocity, or one-sided benefit.
- Check whether any imbalance is clearly justified by prior favors, agreement, or role-based obligation.
- Answer with fairness judgment, not with a proposed compromise or apology plan.

## Special Case
**Implicit favor debt**: Use this unit when no explicit contract exists but one person clearly owes a return favor or shared burden by social norm.

Scene: Sam always drives Lee to work, but Lee never offers gas money or helps when Sam needs a ride.
Question: Is Lee being fair?
Answer logic: The key variable is reciprocity: Lee receives repeated benefit without comparable return, so the exchange is unfair.

## Boundary Exit Rule
- Exit to negotiation if the question is how to divide something acceptably or what compromise to offer.
- Exit to harm/responsibility if the question is who caused damage or who deserves blame.
- Exit to trust/reputation if the question is whether someone is generally reliable over time.
- Exit to faux pas if the issue is social awkwardness, tact, or offense rather than exchange balance.
