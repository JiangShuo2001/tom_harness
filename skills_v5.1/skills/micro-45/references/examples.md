# micro-45 Examples and Boundaries

## Decision Variable
long-term reliability

## Route Signal
Use when the answer depends on whether a person is dependable, loyal, honest, or consistent across past interactions.

## Hard Boundary
- Interpersonal history is required.
- The target is the person's reliability, not the truth of a claim.
- A repeated pattern must matter more than one isolated event.

## Shortcut To Avoid
- Do not treat a believable speaker as a trustworthy person.
- Do not confuse one good deed with a repaired reputation.
- Do not infer trust from politeness, status, or confidence alone.

## Common Failure Modes
- Confusing trust in a person with belief in a statement.
- Overweighting the most recent action and ignoring the longer pattern.
- Using moral approval instead of reliability.
- Importing fairness or blame judgments into a trust decision.

## Minimal Pair
- Case: A friend has repeatedly kept secrets and arrived when they said they would. | Why: The question is about whether their past behavior predicts future reliability.
- Case: A news article has been accurate in the past. | Why: This is about the trustworthiness of a source or claim, which is outside interpersonal reputation.

## Boundary Stress Test
- If the person is nice but frequently lies, lower trust.
- If the person is rude but consistently keeps commitments, reliability can still be high.
- If the prompt asks whether to believe a message, do not score reputation unless the interpersonal history is the key evidence.

## Generalization Note
Generalize from repeated relationship behavior, not from single events, and keep the judgment anchored to future dependability in social interaction.
