# micro-46 Examples and Boundaries

## Decision Variable
the role structure between partners

## Route Signal
Use this micro unit when the narrow diagnostic question is: what role, rank, authority, expertise, or status relation exists between the people?

## Hard Boundary
- This unit identifies the structure; it does not generate a tactful response.
- This unit does not evaluate moral fairness except where role duties define who has authority or obligation.
- This unit does not infer loyalty, reliability, or betrayal from relationship history.
- This unit does not decide disclosure boundaries, though roles may help explain why a privacy expectation exists.

## Shortcut To Avoid
- Do not assume the louder, older, richer, or more confident person has authority unless the scene gives a role-based reason.
- Do not collapse expertise into trustworthiness; an expert role indicates knowledge authority, not necessarily honesty or goodwill.
- Do not treat friendliness as equality when formal evaluation, permission, or supervision remains active.

## Common Failure Modes
- Answering with advice about how to speak to a boss instead of identifying the boss/subordinate structure.
- Confusing role power with personal preference or popularity.
- Mistaking group identity, such as being from the same club or culture, for hierarchical role structure.
- Ignoring temporary situational authority, such as a team captain, meeting chair, event host, or safety officer.
- Over-reading status from stereotypes rather than explicit evidence in the scene.

## Minimal Pair
- Case: A nurse explains medication instructions to a patient who is unsure what to do. | Why: The key variable is an expert/care-provider role relative to a dependent patient.
- Case: A friend who has lied several times gives medication advice from a website. | Why: This is a near neighbor but routes away: the key issue is reliability and source trust, not a stable role structure.

## Boundary Stress Test
- A CEO and an intern play on the same weekend soccer team: identify whether the workplace hierarchy is active in the current scene or whether they are temporary teammates.
- A junior engineer corrects a senior manager about a technical bug: separate organizational rank from domain expertise.
- A teacher asks a student for help fixing a projector: the student may have technical expertise, but the teacher still holds classroom authority.
- A customer yells at a cashier: customer status gives service-role expectations, not unlimited authority over the employee.

## Generalization Note
Role structure is a social-cognitive frame for interpreting obligations, authority, deference, permission, evaluation, and expertise. It should be diagnosed from explicit roles and situational powers, then handed off if the task requires communication strategy, privacy judgment, trust evaluation, or repair.
