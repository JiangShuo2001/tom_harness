# Power, Status, and Role

## Use When
- Use when the question asks who has authority, rank, expertise, responsibility, or role-based control in an interaction.
- Use when labels such as teacher/student, manager/employee, doctor/patient, expert/novice, host/guest, parent/child, officer/citizen, or senior/junior shape how the scene is interpreted.
- Use when the answer depends on whether the relation is hierarchical, peer-to-peer, advisory, supervisory, service-based, or dependency-based.
- Use when a social norm follows from position or role rather than from personal liking, fairness, trust history, or group identity.

## Do Not Use When
- Do not use to decide the best wording, tone, apology, escalation, or persuasion strategy; route to a communication or repair unit instead.
- Do not use when the main variable is whether someone is reliable over time; use Trust and Reputation.
- Do not use when the main variable is whether a resource split, repayment, or burden is fair; use Fairness and Reciprocity.
- Do not use when the main variable is in-group, out-group, stereotype, belonging, or identity membership; use Group Identity.
- Do not use when the main variable is whether information should be kept private or disclosed; use Privacy, Secrets, and Boundaries.

## Decision Variable
the role structure between partners

## Trigger Checklist
- Are there explicit role labels such as boss, teacher, doctor, client, officer, parent, expert, trainee, host, or guest?
- Does one person have formal authority to evaluate, permit, assign, discipline, grade, hire, pay, treat, or enforce?
- Does one person have recognized expertise or gatekeeping power even without formal rank?
- Does the question ask how status, rank, role obligation, or institutional position changes interpretation?
- Would the answer remain mostly the same if personal friendship, liking, or prior trust history were removed?

## Workflow
- List the interaction partners and any explicit titles, roles, institutional settings, or task responsibilities.
- Identify the source of asymmetry: formal authority, expertise, dependency, seniority, service role, caregiving role, or situational control.
- Classify the relation as hierarchical, expert-novice, caregiver-dependent, service-provider/client, host-guest, peer, or unclear/mixed.
- Separate role structure from behavior quality: a rude manager is still a manager; a kind teacher is still an evaluator.
- State only the role-based relation and the norm it activates, such as deference, duty of care, evaluation power, permission control, confidentiality expectation, or obligation to follow instructions.
- If the question asks what message to send or how to repair the interaction, hand off after identifying the role structure.

## Special Case
**Informal familiarity does not erase formal role**: When people are friendly or familiar but one still holds institutional authority or expert responsibility, preserve the formal role unless the scene explicitly suspends it.

Scene: A student jokes casually with her professor after class, then asks whether she can submit the assignment late.
Question: What role structure matters here?
Answer logic: Even though the tone is friendly, the professor still has evaluator and permission-granting authority over the student. The relevant structure is teacher/student with formal academic authority.

## Boundary Exit Rule
- If the task asks what the lower-status or higher-status person should say, exit to a communication, persuasion, or power-distance strategy unit after naming the role.
- If the task asks whether the person deserves trust because of past actions, exit to Trust and Reputation.
- If the task asks whether someone took advantage, owed repayment, or split benefits unfairly, exit to Fairness and Reciprocity.
- If the task asks whether an identity category, stereotype, or belonging explains interpretation, exit to Group Identity.
- If the task asks whether a secret, medical fact, salary, grade, or personal detail may be shared, exit to Privacy, Secrets, and Boundaries.
