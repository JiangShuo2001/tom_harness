# Audience Calibration

## Use When
- Use when the task is to judge whether the wording fits a particular listener's expertise, age, group membership, or prior stance.
- Use when the key issue is whether the speaker adjusted explanation depth, vocabulary, examples, or framing for that audience.
- Use when the question is about listener-fit rather than influence, deception, or offense.

## Do Not Use When
- Do not use when the main question is how to change the listener's belief, attitude, or behavior; that is persuasion strategy.
- Do not use when the main question is whether the statement is misleading, true, false, or selectively framed; that is about deception or implicature.
- Do not use when the main question is whether the speaker is being polite, face-saving, or testy; that is speaker motivation.
- Do not use when the question is about tact, embarrassment, faux pas, or offense; that is social norm violation, not audience fit.

## Decision Variable
how well the expression fits the listener's knowledge, identity, or stance

## Trigger Checklist
- Is the listener an expert, novice, child, insider, outsider, ally, or opponent?
- Does the wording match the listener's likely background knowledge and vocabulary?
- Does the framing respect the listener's identity or position without changing the truth content question?
- Would a different audience need a different level of detail, example, or tone?

## Workflow
- Identify who the target listener is and what they likely know, value, or resist.
- Check whether the utterance is adjusted for that listener's comprehension or stance.
- Ignore whether the speaker is trying to persuade, deceive, or be polite unless that is the explicit question.
- Choose the option whose wording best matches the audience profile.

## Special Case
**Mixed Audience**: If the scene has more than one listener, judge the fit for the explicitly targeted listener or the main audience named in the question; do not average across all listeners.

Scene: A doctor explains a treatment first to a medical intern and then repeats it for a patient using simpler terms.
Question: Which explanation is better calibrated to the listener?
Answer logic: The patient version is better calibrated for a novice listener because it lowers jargon and uses clearer, more accessible wording.

## Boundary Exit Rule
- Exit to persuasion strategy if the core question becomes how to change the listener's mind or behavior.
- Exit to misleading/selective expression if the question is about hidden implications or false inference, not audience fit.
- Exit to speaker motivation if the question is why the speaker chose that wording.
- Exit to faux pas/offense if the issue is embarrassment, rudeness, or violation of social tact.
