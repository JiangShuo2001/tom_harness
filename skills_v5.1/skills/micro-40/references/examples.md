# micro-40 Examples and Boundaries

## Decision Variable
how well the expression fits the listener's knowledge, identity, or stance

## Route Signal
Use this micro unit when the scene asks whether speech is adapted to a listener's expertise, identity, age, or stance.

## Hard Boundary
- Not about changing the listener's belief or behavior.
- Not about whether the message is true, false, or misleading.
- Not about whether the speaker is polite, sarcastic, or face-saving.
- Not about whether the listener is offended or embarrassed.

## Shortcut To Avoid
- Do not treat every tailored message as persuasion; adaptation can be neutral or informative.
- Do not infer motive from audience-fit alone.
- Do not collapse audience calibration into social tact or group politeness.

## Common Failure Modes
- Confusing audience fit with persuasion strategy.
- Confusing audience fit with indirectness or implication.
- Answering from the speaker's motive instead of the listener's needs.
- Overweighting tone while ignoring the audience's knowledge level.

## Minimal Pair
- Case: Explaining a new app to a child using simple words and examples. | Why: The key variable is whether the explanation fits a young listener's comprehension level.
- Case: Explaining the same app to a hesitant customer in vivid terms specifically chosen to make them sign up. | Why: Near miss: the wording is altered to change the listener's decision, not just to fit their knowledge. Route to persuasion strategy.

## Boundary Stress Test
- A speaker simplifies a message to make the listener agree more easily: if influence is the goal, this is persuasion, not audience calibration.
- A speaker says something vaguely to hide a criticism: if the issue is hidden meaning or false inference, this is not audience calibration.
- A speaker changes wording to avoid insulting an out-group: if tact or offense is central, this exits to social norm judgment.

## Generalization Note
Generalize by checking listener fit across knowledge, identity, and stance; do not require a specific benchmark format or a particular social setting.
