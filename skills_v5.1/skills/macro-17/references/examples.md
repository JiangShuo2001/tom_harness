# macro-17 Examples and Boundaries

## Decision Variable
how to acknowledge, compensate, and restore interaction after relationship damage

## Route Signal
The scene contains damage, hurt, offense, or awkwardness and asks how to apologize, compensate, or make things right.

## Hard Boundary
- No active relationship injury means no routing here.
- If the scene is only about clarifying what was meant, this is not the best fit.
- If the scene is mainly about mutually acceptable trade-offs, this is not the best fit.

## Shortcut To Avoid
- Do not jump to explanation when the better first move is acknowledgment of harm.
- Do not treat repair as simple politeness; the relationship state has changed.
- Do not confuse apology with persuasion or negotiation.

## Common Failure Modes
- Answering with a factual clarification instead of a repair move.
- Overusing excuses and underweighting responsibility.
- Giving a compromise answer when the scene needs remorse and restitution.
- Treating the problem as reputation tracking rather than immediate reconciliation.

## Minimal Pair
- Case: A person says something confusing, and the listener asks what they meant. | Why: This is live misunderstanding clarification, not apology after damage.
- Case: A person insults a friend, then wants to know how to fix the relationship. | Why: This is direct apology and repair after relational harm.

## Boundary Stress Test
- If there is no offense or hurt, do not route here.
- If the main task is choosing wording for a polite correction, route elsewhere.
- If the scene includes apology but the actual question is how to divide costs or concessions, route to negotiation.

## Generalization Note
This macro covers real repair behavior across apology, restitution, reassurance, and reconciliation. It should solve any scene where the primary social goal is to restore a damaged relationship.
