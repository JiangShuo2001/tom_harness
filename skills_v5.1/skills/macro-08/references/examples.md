# macro-08 Examples and Boundaries

## Decision Variable
how an utterance operates across truth value, motive, and misleading effect

## Route Signal
The scene asks whether a speaker lied, told a half-truth, gave a cover story, or used wording to create a false impression.

## Hard Boundary
- Ordinary indirect meaning without deception is not enough.
- Pure persuasion without deception is not enough.
- Pure preference or negotiation scenes are not enough.
- Emotion or blame scenes are not enough unless deception is the core.

## Shortcut To Avoid
- Do not collapse every vague or polite statement into a lie.
- Do not treat every misleading listener belief as intentional deception if the utterance is simply indirect.
- Do not answer from surface wording alone without checking reality and speaker knowledge.

## Common Failure Modes
- Calling a polite hint a lie when the speaker only meant an indirect request or warning.
- Missing a half-truth because the literal words are factually correct.
- Focusing on listener misunderstanding while ignoring the speaker's intent.
- Using relationship or emotion logic when the key issue is truthfulness.

## Minimal Pair
- Case: A person says, 'I was not at the office,' when they were there earlier that day and they know it. | Why: The statement is false and the speaker's knowledge makes it a direct lie.
- Case: A person says, 'I already handled it,' meaning only that they sent one email, while knowing the other person will think the whole job is done. | Why: The utterance is technically compatible with one fact but is selected to mislead through selective disclosure.

## Boundary Stress Test
- If the utterance is true but still deceptive, stay in this macro.
- If the utterance is indirect but not deceptive, leave this macro.
- If the main issue is whether the listener should infer a request or warning, leave this macro.
- If the main issue is whether the listener should trust the speaker's reliability over time, consider a trust or relationship macro instead.

## Generalization Note
This macro covers direct lies, strategic omission, bluffing, cover stories, and technically true statements that are designed to mislead, so long as truth status and misleading effect are central.
