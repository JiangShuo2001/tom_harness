# micro-47 Examples and Boundaries

## Decision Variable
whether group membership changes interpretation or evaluation

## Route Signal
Use this micro unit when group membership, in-group/out-group status, stereotypes, or belonging changes how a person, act, or message is interpreted.

## Hard Boundary
- Identity boundary, not power structure.
- Group-based evaluation, not trust from past conduct.
- Belonging effect, not privacy or repair.

## Shortcut To Avoid
- Do not confuse 'they are the boss' with 'they are one of us.'
- Do not infer trust or fault just because a group label is present.
- Do not route scenes about hidden information or apology here unless identity-based judgment is central.

## Common Failure Modes
- Treating role hierarchy as group identity.
- Treating stereotype-based inference as simple attitude or emotion reading.
- Overrouting any social scene with a group label, even when the real variable is trust, privacy, or repair.

## Minimal Pair
- Case: A manager gives the same criticism to two employees, and one is treated as being 'just strict' because of their role. | Why: This is about role and authority, not in-group or out-group identity.
- Case: A new neighbor is judged as unfriendly because they belong to the rival local club. | Why: The judgment is shaped by group membership and out-group bias.

## Boundary Stress Test
- If the same behavior would be judged differently solely because of group membership, this matches.
- If removing the group label removes the explanation, this matches.
- If the scene still works the same after removing identity cues, do not use this unit.

## Generalization Note
This unit covers any identity-bound shift in interpretation or evaluation, including stereotypes, belonging, affiliation, and in-group/out-group bias, as long as the decisive variable is group membership rather than role, trust, privacy, or repair.
