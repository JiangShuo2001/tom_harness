# Group Identity

## Use When
- Use when the question asks how belonging to an in-group or out-group changes interpretation, judgment, or expectations.
- Use when stereotypes, group labels, affiliation, or social identity shift how an act, remark, or person is evaluated.
- Use when the key issue is whether group membership itself explains the observer's inference.
- Use when the task is to identify identity-bound interpretation rather than role-based authority or interpersonal trust.

## Do Not Use When
- Do not use when the main issue is power, status, authority, or role structure; use Power, Status, and Role instead.
- Do not use when the main issue is trustworthiness based on past behavior; use Trust and Reputation instead.
- Do not use when the issue is whether information may be shared or kept private; use Privacy, Secrets, and Boundaries instead.
- Do not use when the issue is repair after offense, apology, or reconciliation; use Relationship Repair and Apology instead.
- Do not use when the question is about literal belief, emotion, intention, or language meaning without any identity-bound inference.

## Decision Variable
whether group membership changes interpretation or evaluation

## Trigger Checklist
- Is the answer driven by in-group versus out-group membership?
- Are stereotypes, belonging, or identity labels affecting judgment?
- Does the scene ask how the same behavior is read differently because of group identity?
- Can the core explanation be stated as an identity-bound bias, expectation, or affiliation effect?

## Workflow
- Identify the relevant group label or membership cue.
- Ask whether the cue changes interpretation, expectation, or evaluation.
- If yes, state the effect of belonging or stereotype on the judgment.
- If the cue instead changes authority, trust, privacy, or repair, exit to the neighboring micro skill.

## Special Case
**Identity cue without explicit stereotype**: If the scene never names a stereotype but still changes judgment because the person is treated as part of a group, this still belongs here.

Scene: A student from the debate team gives a short answer in class, and the teacher assumes the student is being strategic rather than unsure.
Question: Why is the answer interpreted that way?
Answer logic: The teacher's interpretation is shaped by group identity and the stereotype attached to that group, so the effect of membership determines the judgment.

## Boundary Exit Rule
- Exit to micro-46 if the main question is about hierarchy, authority, or formal role rather than group identity.
- Exit to micro-45 if the main question is about whether someone is reliable based on history rather than belonging.
- Exit to micro-48 if the question is about what information may be disclosed or concealed.
- Exit to micro-49 if the question is about apology, harm repair, or restoring the relationship after offense.
