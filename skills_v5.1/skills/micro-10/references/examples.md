# micro-10 Examples and Boundaries

## Decision Variable
whether the character has the background required to interpret the information

## Route Signal
Use this micro unit when the scene hinges on whether a person can correctly interpret information because of missing background knowledge, expertise, or cultural context.

## Hard Boundary
- Not perception: the person may have clearly heard or seen the information.
- Not memory: the person may once have known it or never learned it at all.
- Not belief truth status: the issue is comprehension, not whether the belief matches reality.
- Not social intention: do not use unless interpretation depends specifically on background competence.

## Shortcut To Avoid
- Do not treat every misunderstanding as false belief.
- Do not infer ignorance from silence, confusion, or surprise unless the missing background is explicit.
- Do not collapse domain expertise into general intelligence.
- Do not use this for word meaning alone if the scene is actually about belief, deception, or memory.

## Common Failure Modes
- Confusing not knowing a term with not believing a fact.
- Choosing memory when the character never had the relevant background in the first place.
- Choosing perception when the character saw the cue but lacked the context to decode it.
- Overgeneralizing from one narrow technical example to all comprehension problems.

## Minimal Pair
- Case: A mechanic understands a dashboard warning light, but a child does not know what it means. | Why: The difference is background knowledge needed to interpret the cue.
- Case: Two people hear the same slang phrase, but only one understands the cultural reference behind it. | Why: The diagnostic variable is shared background knowledge, not hearing or belief.

## Boundary Stress Test
- The character heard the sentence perfectly, but cannot explain what it means.
- The character saw the symbol, but lacks the training to decode it.
- The character is confused because the reference depends on local culture or profession, not because they forgot it.

## Generalization Note
This unit generalizes to any prior-knowledge gap that blocks interpretation, including professional, cultural, experiential, and conceptual background.
