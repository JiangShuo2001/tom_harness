# macro-09 Examples and Boundaries

## Decision Variable
how to adapt expression to a listener and change their belief, attitude, or behavior

## Route Signal
The scene asks how to phrase, frame, explain, advise, encourage, warn, or persuade a particular listener so the listener will change belief, attitude, or behavior.

## Hard Boundary
- Not for negotiation, compromise, or joint decision making.
- Not for pure literal comprehension or indirect speech alone.
- Not for truth evaluation alone.
- Not for cases where the listener is irrelevant to the answer.

## Shortcut To Avoid
- Do not treat directness as automatically best.
- Do not ignore whether the listener is skeptical, expert, novice, defensive, or motivated.
- Do not answer as if the speaker is talking to a generic audience.

## Common Failure Modes
- Giving the factually best answer instead of the listener-best answer.
- Confusing persuasion with negotiation because both involve social strategy.
- Ignoring the listener's expertise gap and using the wrong level of explanation.
- Missing the difference between informing someone and changing what they will do.

## Minimal Pair
- Case: A doctor explains a treatment to a worried patient in simple terms. | Why: The key variable is adapting the explanation to the listener so they accept and follow the advice.
- Case: Two coworkers discuss salary offers and trade concessions. | Why: This is not one-way influence; it is mutual bargaining and belongs outside this macro.

## Boundary Stress Test
- If the listener already agrees, ask whether the remaining task is still to adapt the message for compliance or merely to restate it.
- If both sides can change offers, it is likely negotiation rather than persuasion.
- If the question is only about what a phrase means literally, do not route here.
- If success depends on trust, status, or pride, check whether the answer is about audience calibration rather than content alone.

## Generalization Note
This macro covers any one-way social influence scene in which the best move is to tailor the message to the audience's barriers, motives, and understanding, whether the goal is advice, encouragement, warning, explanation, or persuasion.
