# Persuasion, Advice, and Audience Calibration

## Use When
- Use when the scene is a one-way influence problem, not a mutual bargaining problem.
- Use when the main task is to choose how to say something so the listener will understand, accept, or do it.
- Use when the speaker must adapt to the listener's knowledge level, resistance, motive, trust, or emotional state.
- Use when the answer is the best message, advice, framing, or angle that moves a specific listener.

## Do Not Use When
- Do not use when the core task is two-way negotiation, compromise, or joint allocation.
- Do not use when the core issue is only indirect meaning, lying, or truth status rather than audience fit and influence.
- Do not use when the main question is simply what was said literally, without any need to adapt to the listener.
- Do not use when another narrower micro capability fully determines the answer and the scene is not primarily about listener-adapted influence.

## Decision Variable
how to adapt expression to a listener and change their belief, attitude, or behavior

## Direct Route Rule
Route immediately when the message must fit the listener's barrier, expertise, or motive.

## Trigger Checklist
- There is a speaker and a target listener.
- The speaker's goal is to influence belief, attitude, or behavior.
- The listener has a barrier such as low knowledge, skepticism, fear, pride, or different priorities.
- The answer depends on phrasing, framing, evidence, tone, timing, or level of detail.
- The best response is adapted to the listener rather than maximally complete or maximally literal.
- The scene is not primarily a negotiation where both sides must trade offers.

## Workflow
- Identify the intended outcome: belief change, attitude shift, or behavior change.
- Identify the listener profile: expertise, motive, resistance, trust level, and likely concern.
- Choose the framing that reduces the listener's barrier while preserving the core message.
- Prefer language that matches the listener's vocabulary, incentives, and emotional state.
- If the scene asks which message will work best, select the one most likely to move the listener.
- If the scene asks why a line was said, infer the influence goal behind the adaptation.

## Special Case
**Advice to a resistant listener**: When the listener already knows the facts but resists the action, solve by optimizing for motivation, face-saving, or trust rather than for information density.

Scene: A friend knows they should apologize, but they feel defensive and do not want to admit fault.
Question: What should you say to get them to consider apologizing?
Answer logic: Do not overload them with facts. Use a low-threat framing that preserves dignity, acknowledges their feelings, and makes the apology seem socially safe and beneficial.

## Boundary Exit Rule
- Exit to macro-18 when the scene requires back-and-forth settlement rather than one-way influence.
- Exit to macro-07 when the key issue is only literal versus implied meaning, with no audience-calibration problem.
- Exit to macro-08 when the main task is to determine whether a statement is true, false, or misleading rather than how to make it effective.
- Exit to macro-14 when the answer hinges on quantity, probability, or sample-based estimation rather than persuasion.
- Add micro-20 when the listener's preferences must be modeled before tailoring the message.
- Add micro-40 when the language must be calibrated to the listener's expertise, identity, or stance.
- Add micro-41 when the scene calls for a specific influence strategy (frame, evidence, social proof, etc.).
- Add micro-46 when status, authority, or institutional role of the listener changes what is permissible.
