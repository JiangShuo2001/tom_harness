# micro-28 Examples and Boundaries

## Decision Variable
internal emotion versus external expression

## Route Signal
Use this micro unit when a scene contrasts a character's private emotional state with a different outward expression or statement.

## Hard Boundary
- This unit detects the mismatch; it does not explain the social rule that caused the masking.
- This unit does not decide whether the character should have hidden the emotion.
- This unit does not classify lies or strategic misdirection unless the lie is only evidence of an emotional display.
- This unit does not infer moral emotions unless the central variable is still the hidden-versus-shown emotion contrast.

## Shortcut To Avoid
- Do not trust the visible smile, calm tone, or verbal 'I'm fine' as the true emotion when private evidence points the other way.
- Do not assume every hidden emotion is sadness; infer the private feeling from the character's goal, threat, loss, or concern.

## Common Failure Modes
- Answering with the displayed emotion instead of the private emotion.
- Explaining politeness rules when the question only asks what the character really feels.
- Treating an unusual but openly expressed emotion as hidden emotion.
- Ignoring later private behavior that reveals the internal state.
- Over-reading concealment when the scene provides no evidence of a mismatch.

## Minimal Pair
- Case: Leo loses a contest he cared about, smiles at the winner, and later tells his brother he feels crushed. | Why: The outward smile conflicts with private disappointment, so the answer depends on hidden emotion.
- Case: Leo loses a contest he cared about and openly cries in front of everyone. | Why: This does not match Hidden Emotion; the internal feeling and external expression align, so Basic Emotion Appraisal is sufficient.

## Boundary Stress Test
- A nurse stays calm during an emergency but is privately scared: use Hidden Emotion if the question asks what she feels; use Display Rules if the question asks why she stays calm.
- A child laughs after losing a toy because she thinks the loss is funny: use Atypical Emotion, not Hidden Emotion, if the laughter reflects the true feeling.
- A student says 'I'm happy for you' after a rival wins, while clenching fists and avoiding eye contact: use Hidden Emotion if the target is the student's real feeling.
- A person hides guilt after breaking a promise: use Moral Emotion if responsibility and guilt are the core inference; use Hidden Emotion only if the main task is contrasting guilt with a neutral display.

## Generalization Note
Across benchmark and real-world cases, separate the evidence for private appraisal from evidence for public display, then answer the diagnostic question by comparing the two.
