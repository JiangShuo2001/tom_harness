# micro-17 Examples and Boundaries

## Decision Variable
the character's subjective certainty

## Route Signal
Use this micro unit when the scene centers on a character's level of conviction, doubt, or guessing rather than on truth, evidence, or source quality.

## Hard Boundary
- This unit does not judge whether the belief is correct.
- This unit does not judge whether the evidence is strong.
- This unit does not judge whether the source is reliable.
- This unit does not track whether other people know the same fact.

## Shortcut To Avoid
- Do not confuse uncertainty with false belief; a wrong belief can still be held confidently.
- Do not confuse doubt with distrust; distrust belongs to source credibility.
- Do not use answer choices about evidence strength when the prompt asks about confidence only.

## Common Failure Modes
- Treating 'maybe' as low evidence instead of low certainty.
- Answering with the fact content instead of the degree of conviction.
- Attributing uncertainty to a bad source when the text only shows hesitation.
- Mixing up partial belief with disbelief.

## Minimal Pair
- Case: A person says, 'I am sure the keys are in the drawer.' | Why: The relevant variable is high subjective certainty.
- Case: A person says, 'I think the keys might be in the drawer, but I am not sure.' | Why: The relevant variable is reduced subjective certainty, not the key location itself.

## Boundary Stress Test
- If the person has weak evidence but sounds fully confident, still prefer source or evidence skills only if the question asks why the belief should change.
- If the person has strong evidence but says 'I am not sure,' this unit remains correct because the target is certainty, not accuracy.
- If the question asks both what the person believes and how sure they are, answer certainty here only when certainty is the asked-for variable.

## Generalization Note
This skill generalizes to any case where confidence is explicitly or implicitly graded, including hedging, tentative acceptance, and self-reported doubt.
