# macro-10 Examples and Boundaries

## Decision Variable
which action, option, or allocation should be chosen when preferences conflict

## Route Signal
A scene with competing wants where the question is what gets chosen, who gets the resource, or what joint action is most likely.

## Hard Boundary
- Negotiation process and concessions are central instead of the final decision.
- Truthfulness, deception, or indirect meaning is central.
- Commitment scheduling or next-action priority is central without a real preference conflict.
- Social embarrassment or apology is the main issue.

## Shortcut To Avoid
- Do not convert every disagreement into bargaining analysis.
- Do not overcomplicate with hidden-belief reasoning when the answer is just the likely preference-based choice.
- Do not treat planning language as automatically separate from the actual choice.
- Do not assume equal compromise when the story clearly signals a dominant preference or constraint.

## Common Failure Modes
- Answering the process of negotiation instead of the final chosen option.
- Using persuasion or pragmatics when the scene is really about allocation.
- Ignoring one person's stronger preference and forcing a symmetric compromise.
- Confusing preference conflict with commitment priority or follow-through.
- Missing simple role-based or ownership-based resolution.

## Minimal Pair
- Case: Two coworkers must decide which lunch spot to pick, and one strongly prefers sushi while the other does not care. | Why: The scene is about the chosen option under unequal preferences.
- Case: A family must divide one dessert, and the question is who should get the larger piece. | Why: The scene is about allocation when preferences conflict.

## Boundary Stress Test
- If the scene includes offers, counteroffers, and concessions, ask whether the real answer is the final allocation or the bargaining process.
- If the scene includes a schedule conflict, ask whether the key issue is commitment priority rather than preference conflict.
- If the scene includes a misleading remark about desire, ask whether truth status or indirect meaning is actually the target.
- If the scene involves a social misstep or apology, route away from this macro.

## Generalization Note
Use this macro whenever a scene can be solved by mapping competing preferences onto a final choice, even if the specific setting is a meal, trip, game, task, gift, or shared resource.
