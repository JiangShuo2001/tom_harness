# Preference Conflict and Joint Decision

## Use When
- Use when the scene asks what action, choice, or allocation should be selected under conflicting preferences.
- Use when two or more people want different things and the key question is which option will be chosen, not how the bargaining unfolds.
- Use when a shared decision, split resource, or next move must be determined from competing wants or priorities.
- Use when the answer depends on preference strength, commitment, ownership, role, or joint benefit under conflict.

## Do Not Use When
- Do not use when the main question is persuasion, audience adaptation, or one-way influence rather than a choice under preference conflict.
- Do not use when the main question is whether an utterance is true, deceptive, or misleading.
- Do not use when the main question is what happens next because of prior commitments or task sequencing rather than preference conflict itself.
- Do not use when the main question is whether a remark caused embarrassment, offense, or social harm.
- Do not use when the scene is primarily about negotiation mechanics, concessions, or bargaining process rather than the final choice or allocation.

## Decision Variable
which action, option, or allocation should be chosen when preferences conflict

## Direct Route Rule
Route immediately when divergent desire, shared desire, or preference-based action prediction is asked.

## Trigger Checklist
- At least two agents have different wants, goals, or preferred outcomes.
- The question asks for a choice, allocation, or predicted selection.
- The answer depends on whose preference matters most, how preferences combine, or what compromise is most likely.
- The scene is about the result of conflicting wants, not the detailed bargaining conversation.
- A shared plan, joint action, or distribution decision is being made.

## Workflow
- Identify the decision target: action, allocation, or joint plan.
- List each relevant person's preference, constraint, or commitment.
- Check whether one preference clearly dominates by role, ownership, urgency, or strength.
- If preferences are balanced, infer the most jointly acceptable compromise or split.
- If the question asks what someone will do, choose the option most consistent with their strongest operative preference.
- State the final chosen action or allocation directly.

## Special Case
**Compromise with unequal preference strength**: When one person cares much more than the other and the scene asks for the likely outcome, favor the option that best satisfies the stronger preference unless the story gives a clear reason for equal sharing or role-based override.

Scene: Two friends are deciding where to eat. One is indifferent, but the other strongly wants pasta and has already suggested a nearby pasta place.
Question: Where will they probably go?
Answer logic: The stronger preference controls the joint decision, so they will probably go to the pasta place.

## Boundary Exit Rule
- Exit to macro-09 if the main issue is how to phrase, frame, or persuade rather than the final choice.
- Exit to macro-08 if the task is whether someone is lying, half-telling, or misleading in wording.
- Exit to macro-11 if the key variable is what happens next because of prior commitments, schedules, or invitations.
- Exit to macro-12 if the scene is mainly about embarrassment, offense, or face threat from a remark.
- Exit to macro-18 if both sides must trade concessions to reach a joint plan rather than have one preference win.
- Add micro-19 when each party's own preference must be diagnosed.
- Add micro-20 when modeling the other party's preference is needed.
- Add micro-21 when the choice depends on subordinate-vs-superordinate goal structure.
- Add micro-23 when the next-action prediction needs explicit treatment.
- Add micro-25 when general goal-conflict arbitration rules apply.
- Add micro-44 when fairness or reciprocity norms shape the acceptable allocation.
