# Social Cues and Nonverbal Intention

## Use When
- Use when the key evidence is a nonverbal cue such as a glance, wink, smile, nod, shrug, gesture, pause, posture, or facial expression.
- Use when the question asks what the cue means, what the sender intended, or what social action the cue performs.
- Use when the scene requires interpreting a signal that is not mainly a spoken statement.
- Use when the core task is to infer communication, invitation, warning, reassurance, disapproval, secrecy, or coordination from bodily or facial behavior.

## Do Not Use When
- Do not use when the main task is the literal meaning of spoken language, indirect speech, implicature, lying, or persuasion.
- Do not use when the main task is hidden emotion rather than the social meaning of the cue itself.
- Do not use when the key issue is false belief, appearance-reality conflict, or knowledge boundary rather than cue interpretation.
- Do not use when the cue is only background detail and the answer depends on moral blame, relationship repair, or power hierarchy instead.

## Decision Variable
what intention or social action a nonverbal cue conveys

## Direct Route Rule
Route immediately when the cue is nonverbal and social inference or sender intention is asked.

## Trigger Checklist
- A cue is nonverbal rather than mainly spoken.
- The question is about meaning, intention, or social function of the cue.
- The answer depends on reading gaze, facial expression, gesture, posture, or timing.
- The cue is used for signaling, coordination, warning, teasing, reassurance, refusal, or invitation.
- The scene can be solved without first resolving a language-pragmatics problem.

## Workflow
- Identify the nonverbal cue and the observer's perspective.
- Ask what social action the cue is performing in context.
- Use relationship, setting, and immediate conversational state to infer sender intent.
- Distinguish between emotion display and communicative signal.
- State the most likely intended message or social act directly.

## Special Case
**Cue looks emotional but functions as a signal**: If the outward display could be read as emotion but the scene asks what it is doing socially, prioritize the communicative function over the emotion label.

Scene: During a group meeting, Priya raises her eyebrows and glances at Sam without speaking.
Question: What is Priya probably trying to communicate?
Answer logic: Do not stop at 'surprised.' The nonverbal cue is serving as a signal to Sam, so infer the intended social message from the shared context, such as 'pay attention,' 'do you agree,' or 'I want you to notice this.'

## Boundary Exit Rule
- Exit to macro-07 if the main evidence is a spoken sentence whose indirect meaning must be decoded.
- Exit to macro-05 if the question is primarily about what the person feels rather than what the cue communicates.
- Exit to macro-01 / macro-02 if the main issue is who knows what, who saw what, or where someone will look.
- Exit to macro-08 if the cue accompanies a deceptive utterance and truth status drives the answer.
- Add micro-07 when attentional capture must be diagnosed (who noticed the cue).
- Add micro-22 when the question is the immediate purpose behind the cue.
- Add micro-23 when the cue is mainly a predictor of the next action.
- Add micro-26 when the cue's affective signal must be separated from its communicative function.
- Add micro-35 when the cue substitutes for a speech act (request, refusal, warning).
