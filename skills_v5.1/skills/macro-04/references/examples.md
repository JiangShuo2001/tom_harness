# macro-04 Examples and Boundaries

## Decision Variable
what intention or social action a nonverbal cue conveys

## Route Signal
The scene centers on a wink, glance, smile, nod, shrug, gesture, posture shift, or pause that carries a social message.

## Hard Boundary
- Not for literal sentence meaning.
- Not for first-order false-belief search questions.
- Not for pure emotion labeling unless the question is specifically about the emotion itself.
- Not for moral judgment, apology, or trust unless the cue is only incidental.

## Shortcut To Avoid
- Do not convert every facial expression into an emotion question.
- Do not assume the cue is meaningless decoration if the question asks why it happened.
- Do not use language-pragmatics reasoning to explain a cue that is primarily nonverbal.
- Do not overfit to one gesture; solve by function in context.

## Common Failure Modes
- Treating a wink or glance as mere emotion instead of intentional signaling.
- Answering with the speaker's literal words when the real signal is nonverbal.
- Confusing hidden emotion with communicated intent.
- Missing that a small cue is a request, warning, joke, or invitation.

## Minimal Pair
- Case: A person says, 'Let's go,' while nodding toward the exit. | Why: The nonverbal cue is part of the intended social action and must be interpreted as a signal to leave.
- Case: A person silently winks at a friend during a tense conversation. | Why: The cue is nonverbal and the task is to infer the social meaning of the wink, such as a private signal or joke.

## Boundary Stress Test
- If the same cue appears but the question asks what the person feels, switch to an emotion macro.
- If the same cue appears but the answer depends on the wording of a sentence, switch to a language-pragmatics macro.
- If the same cue appears but the key issue is who noticed it, switch to a perception or knowledge macro.
- If the same cue appears but the main issue is offense, embarrassment, or repair, switch to the relevant social-norm macro.

## Generalization Note
This macro covers reusable social signal interpretation, not memorization of specific gestures. The invariant is that a nonverbal cue is being used as a communicative act whose meaning must be inferred from context.
