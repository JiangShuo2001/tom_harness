# UO-01 Reference

## Decision Variable

- The key variable is the character-specific appraisal override (or, for
  hidden-emotion items, the SOURCE EVENT's valence for this character),
  not the default everyday emotional script.

## Route Signal

- Use this skill when the question asks which emotion fits after
  checking whether a trait, goal, value, relationship, or hiding action
  overrides the generic response.

## Hard Boundary

- If the task asks why the surprising emotion happened, route to
  `skill9`.
- If the task is moral emotion after a possibly-harmful action AND any
  anti-guilt cue is present (young child, "MIGHT have", self-justifying
  good-deed framing, external reassurance, explicit indifference), route
  to `skill22`.
- If there is no meaningful override cue and the story is just a
  standard reaction, do not force this skill.

## Shortcut To Avoid

- Do not answer from average-person intuition alone.
- Do not let threat words force fear or betrayal words force resentment
  before checking character-specific cues.
- Do not default to "anxiety about being caught" for hidden-emotion
  items.

## Common Failure Modes

- Choosing a broad default emotion before examining override evidence.
- Missing moral self-appraisal cues that support guilt, regret, or
  embarrassment.
- Ignoring role, relationship history, or prior arrangement that
  changes the emotional meaning of the event.
- Picking "fear of being discovered" as the real feeling for someone
  hiding a positive secret like good cards.

## Minimal Pair

- Case A: an ordinary person hears footsteps at night and feels fear.
- Case B: a brave, curiosity-driven person in the same setup may feel
  curiosity instead.
- Case C (hidden-emotion): a child holds good cards but keeps a
  poker-face — the real feeling is excited, not anxious.

## Boundary Stress Test

- `What emotion does she have?` with a strong override cue → this skill.
- `She should feel betrayed, but why does she feel regret?` → `skill9`.
- `What does Xiaoming feel after he fed the rabbits carrots that turned
  out to be too many?` (with the zookeeper saying "rabbits ate too
  much") → `skill22`.

## Generalization Note

- The transferable core is appraisal-based emotion reasoning: emotion
  follows how the character construes the event (or the SOURCE EVENT
  they are hiding), not just the visible event itself.
