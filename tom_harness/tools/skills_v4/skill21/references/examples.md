# CP-01 Reference

## Decision Variable

- The key variable is which level of the priority hierarchy
  ((a) prior commitment > (b) ongoing activity > (c) new invitation >
  (d) background task) dominates the next action.

## Route Signal

- Use this skill when the question is "what does X do NEXT" with
  several time-ordered claims competing.

## Hard Boundary

- If the task is who should adapt to whose preference, route to
  `skill18`.
- If the task is what to say to convince the other party, route to
  `skill13`.

## Shortcut To Avoid

- Do not over-weight the freshest message or latest distraction.
- Do not abandon the dominant commitment without explicit story
  evidence.

## Common Failure Modes

- Picking the new-invitation option just because it appeared most
  recently in the story.
- Forgetting that a freshly-made verbal promise dominates a fresh
  distraction.
- Missing the failed-action replacement rule (same underlying goal
  survives even after the original activity becomes impossible).

## Minimal Pair

- Case A: ongoing activity (just refused a distraction) > new
  invitation → continues activity.
- Case B: prior promise to help a friend at 3pm > everything else →
  goes to help.

## Boundary Stress Test

- `What does Li Hua do after the basketball invitation?` → this
  skill, ongoing > new.
- `Where should the husband take his wife for their anniversary?` →
  `skill18`.

## Generalization Note

- The transferable core is priority arbitration over time-ordered
  commitments: the deeper goal usually dominates the freshest
  distraction.
