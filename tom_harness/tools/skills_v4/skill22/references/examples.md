# ME-01 Reference

## Decision Variable

- The key variable is whether ANY of the 5 anti-guilt checks fires
  (age / uncertainty / self-justifying good-deed / external
  reassurance / explicit indifference). For belief-driven cases, the
  variable is the character's BELIEF state (favoured / rejected /
  wronged / hopeless).

## Route Signal

- Use this skill when the question is moral-emotion-after-action AND
  at least one anti-guilt cue is present, OR when the character's
  emotion is clearly tied to their BELIEF rather than to objective
  reality.

## Hard Boundary

- If no anti-guilt cue and no belief-vs-reality split is present,
  route to `skill8`.
- If the question is explicitly "why does X feel Y instead of Z",
  route to `skill9`.
- If the question is about truth status of a statement, route to
  `skill14`.

## Shortcut To Avoid

- Do not default to guilt / panic without checking the 5 anti-guilt
  cues.
- Do not impose the reader's morally-correct outsider feeling on the
  character.

## Common Failure Modes

- Picking guilt for a 2-year-old who has no concept of harm.
- Picking nervousness for "X realises he MIGHT have made a mistake"
  (the cue is uncertainty, not nervousness).
- Picking guilt when the actor was trying to do a good deed and the
  harm is indirect.
- Picking guilt when a third party explicitly absolves the actor.
- Picking guilt when the story explicitly says "doesn't bother to
  return / not her responsibility".

## Minimal Pair

- Case A: Yuki believes she is safe from layoffs → peace of mind, not
  panic.
- Case B: Xiao Qiang fed the rabbits "good" food and the keeper said
  "the rabbits ate too much" → satisfied / proud (good-deed framing),
  not guilt.
- Case C: same scene but the keeper says "your specific carrots made
  them sick" with no other anti-guilt cue → guilt.

## Boundary Stress Test

- `What does Xiao Qiang feel after feeding the rabbits a lot?` →
  this skill, Check 3 fires.
- `What does the 2-year-old feel after breaking the vase?` → this
  skill, Check 1 fires.
- `What does Roy feel after the school ignored his application?` →
  this skill, belief-driven branch (rejected → hurt / irritated).
- `What does Sara feel after losing the job?` (no anti-guilt cue, no
  belief-vs-reality split) → `skill8`.

## Generalization Note

- The transferable core is resisting moral-intuition shortcuts:
  emotion follows the character's BELIEF and the explicit story cues,
  not the reader's outside moral judgement.
